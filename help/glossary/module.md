A module is the basic unit of a PAT firm assessment: a set of twenty questions examining one domain of a firm's operations. The core assessment consists of five modules — one hundred questions in total — and each module feeds one of PAT's five pillars. Beyond the core five, short adaptive modules can open based on a firm's scoring pattern, at concept level, to probe, explain, or guide.

## What a module is

A module in PAT is a bounded set of twenty assessment questions addressed to one domain of firm operations. The firm assessment contains five such modules, for one hundred questions in total. Each core module corresponds to a named domain and feeds a named pillar: Operating Model & Workflow Discipline feeds the Operations pillar; Automation & AI Readiness feeds Automation; Integration & Data Flow Maturity feeds Integration; Governance, Controls & Vendor Risk feeds Governance; and Strategy, Change Readiness & Market Alignment feeds Strategy.

A distinctive feature of the module design is that the same twenty questions repeat in every module, on purpose. The wording is identical across all five; what changes is the context the module supplies. When a question refers to "this area," the module's domain defines what that area is. Within Integration & Data Flow Maturity, "this area" means the firm's integration and data-flow practice; within Governance, Controls & Vendor Risk, the same words mean its governance practice. The module, in other words, is not just a container of questions — it is the frame that gives a shared question its specific meaning.

## Why modules exist

Modules exist to divide a broad question — how well is this firm operating? — into domains that can be examined one at a time and read side by side. A single undifferentiated questionnaire would blur where a firm is strong and where it is not. Five modules, each aimed at one domain, produce five distinct scores a firm can compare.

The deliberate repetition of question wording serves that comparison. Because every module asks the identical twenty questions, differences between a firm's five scores reflect differences in the domains themselves, not differences in how questions happened to be phrased. Identical wording is what makes the five scores comparable; the module supplies the context that keeps each answer anchored to its own domain. A firm answering the assessment is therefore doing something more structured than filling out five separate surveys — it is applying one consistent measuring instrument to five areas of its own practice.

## How modules work

Questions within a module are answered on a consistent numeric scale, and those scored answers feed PAT's deterministic scoring arithmetic, which produces results on a 0–100 scale. No AI model sits anywhere in the scoring path; the same answers always produce the same score. Results present with five bands — Early, Developing, Building, Established, Leading — with the band leading the presentation and the raw number supporting it.

Not everything inside a module carries score weight. Open-ended questions carry zero score weight: they are qualitative only. A firm's written responses add texture and context to what the numeric answers show, but they never move a score up or down. This is a structural rule, not a matter of grading judgment — since scoring is deterministic arithmetic over scaled numeric answers, there is simply no path by which free-text content enters a score.

Beyond the five core modules, PAT includes the concept of adaptive modules: additional short modules that can open based on a firm's scoring pattern. There are three types. A diagnostic module probes a weak area in more depth. A strength module explores why something is working, so the firm can understand a strong result rather than merely possess it. A remediation module offers guided improvement. Adaptive modules are defined at concept level, and all adaptive-module content is reviewed and sourced before it can serve — nothing opens for a firm that has not passed that review.

## Inside a module: sections and the firm's own words

A module has internal shape. Modules contain sections, and each of the five modules covers its pillar through the same twenty scored questions plus a small set of open-ended questions. The two kinds of item do different work. The scored twenty, answered on a consistent numeric scale, are what the deterministic arithmetic consumes; they are the measuring instrument. The open-ended items are qualitative and carry zero score weight — their job is to give context in the firm's own words. Where a numeric answer records how a firm rates an aspect of a domain, an open-ended answer records what the firm would actually say about it: the situation behind the rating, described by the people living it.

That division of labor is deliberate. Because scoring is deterministic arithmetic over scaled numeric answers, the firm's written words can be candid without consequence — nothing a firm writes can raise or lower a score, so there is no incentive to write for effect. The open-ended items make a module more than a scoring device: the numbers make five domains comparable, and the words keep each domain's picture recognizably the firm's own.

## Completing a module: pacing, autosave, and order

Working through modules is designed to fit a firm's real calendar rather than demand a single sitting. The assessment experience is a paged flow with autosave: a firm can stop partway through a module and resume later without losing work. Progress is tracked per module, so a firm always knows which of the five modules are complete, which are underway, and which are untouched.

Order is the firm's choice. Modules can be completed in any order over time — a firm might begin with the domain it knows best, or with the one it most wants examined, and there is no penalty or distortion either way, because every module asks the same twenty questions and the scoring arithmetic is indifferent to sequence. Results update as modules complete: a firm that has finished three of five modules already sees results for those three domains rather than waiting on an all-or-nothing reveal. The assessment is one instrument, but it is administered at the firm's pace, module by module.

## Modules over time: re-assessment and cadence

A module's score is a reading at a point in time, not a permanent label. Assessments can be re-taken as operations change, and PAT treats re-assessment as the normal life of the instrument rather than an admission that the first reading failed. The intended long-term reading is band movement: a firm re-assessing across quarters can see whether an area moved — from Developing to Building, say — with bands leading every presentation and the raw 0–100 numbers in support. Because the same twenty questions repeat in every module and scoring is deterministic, a change between two readings of the same module reflects a change in the firm, not a change in the yardstick.

PAT paces this engagement deliberately. The platform's cadence philosophy favors steady rhythms — quarterly, for instance — and its reminders are no-guilt by design: informative nudges, never shame mechanics. A reminder that a module sits unfinished or that a re-assessment window has arrived is information about timing, not a judgment about diligence. The aim is a sustainable rhythm in which modules are revisited because operations changed and the firm wants a fresh reading, not because a mechanism made them feel behind.

## Design rationale — why modules were built this way

Two design decisions shape what a module is, and each was made deliberately.

The first is that a module's score is a point-in-time reading, never a permanent label. The reasoning is blunt: operations change. A firm that reworks its data flows, tightens its controls, or restructures how work moves through the practice is no longer the firm that answered the questions last quarter, and a measurement system that kept describing it with last quarter's number would be describing a firm that no longer exists. A permanent label would therefore be false — not harsh, false. The honest design is re-assessment on a working rhythm: treat each module result as a dated reading, expect modules to be retaken as operations move, and let the meaningful signal accumulate across readings rather than reside in any single one. This is why band movement over time is the intended long-term reading of the instrument, and why the platform's cadence philosophy paces re-assessment with quarterly rhythms and no-guilt reminders instead of treating a first score as a verdict to be defended.

The second decision is that open-ended questions carry zero score weight, and the reasoning protects two different goods at once. Qualitative context should inform human judgment — a partner's paragraph about a data-migration headache tells a human reader things no numeric answer can. But it should not sway arithmetic. The moment free text influenced a score, the five module scores would stop being strictly comparable, because each would partly reflect how articulately an answer happened to be written rather than what the domain is actually like. Zero weight resolves the tension cleanly: scores stay comparable, computed by the same deterministic arithmetic over the same scaled numeric answers in every module, while the firm's own words are preserved intact for the readers — human ones — they were written for. The two decisions reinforce each other: a repeatable instrument produces readings worth comparing across time, and words kept out of the arithmetic keep every one of those readings clean.

## A worked example

The firm in this example is invented for illustration. Harbor & Vale Accountants works through the firm assessment: five modules, twenty questions each, one hundred questions in all, answered at the firm's own pace. The firm chooses its own order, starting with Governance, Controls & Vendor Risk because a partner has vendor contracts on her mind that month, and leaving Automation & AI Readiness for last. Midway through the second module a client emergency interrupts; the paged flow has been autosaving, so the half-finished module waits, its progress tracked, and the partner resumes a week later exactly where she stopped. As each module completes, its result appears — by the time three modules are done, Harbor & Vale is already reading three domain results rather than waiting for the full hundred questions.

Because the twenty questions are identical from module to module, the partners notice that the questions feel familiar by the third module — which is exactly the design. Their answers about "this area" mean workflow discipline in the first module and data-flow maturity in the third, and the differences in their answers are differences in the firm, not in the questionnaire.

When scoring completes, Harbor & Vale sees five results, one per pillar, each presented band-first with the 0–100 number in support. Suppose its Integration result lands at 62 while its Governance result lands notably higher. In an open-ended item, a partner had written a paragraph about a recent data-migration headache; that paragraph enriches the qualitative picture in the firm's own words, but it contributed nothing to the 62, because open-ended items carry zero score weight. Based on the firm's scoring pattern, a short diagnostic module could open to probe the integration weakness in more depth, and a strength module could open against the strong governance result to help the firm understand why that area works. If a remediation module later serves guided improvement content, that content will have been reviewed and sourced before Harbor & Vale ever sees it.

The story does not end with the first reading. Over the following quarter the firm reworks its data flows, and when a no-guilt reminder notes that a re-assessment window has arrived — a nudge, not a scolding — the partners retake the Integration & Data Flow Maturity module. The same twenty questions, the same deterministic arithmetic, a changed firm: the new reading lands higher, and the band presentation shows Integration moving from Developing to Building. That movement between two readings of one module is the long-term reading PAT intends — the score of a module treated as a point-in-time measurement, repeated as operations change, with band motion telling the story across quarters.

## What a module is not

A module is not a pillar. The module is the assessment unit — the twenty questions and the domain context in which they are answered — while the pillar is the scoring construct the module feeds. Related, but not interchangeable.

A module is not a bespoke questionnaire per topic. The repetition of identical questions across all five modules is not an oversight or a shortcut; it is the mechanism that makes the five results comparable, and treating it as filler misreads the design. A module is also not partially machine-scored: no AI model participates in scoring, so nothing in a module's result reflects a model's judgment. Open-ended items within a module are not scored at all — a thoughtful essay answer and a blank one produce the same score contribution, which is zero.

A module is likewise not a one-sitting exam or a deadline mechanism. The paged, autosaving flow expects interruption; modules can be finished in any order over time; and the reminders that pace the work are informative nudges, never shame mechanics. Nor is a module's score a permanent label — it is a point-in-time reading of a domain, meant to be retaken as operations change.

Finally, adaptive modules are not penalties, and they are not improvised. A diagnostic module opening against a weak area is a deeper look, not a sanction; a strength module is not a reward but an explanation; and none of the three types can serve content that has not been reviewed and sourced first.

## Common misconceptions about modules

One misconception holds that because the same twenty questions appear in all five modules, completing one module makes the other four redundant — that a firm's remaining answers could be predicted or carried over. In fact each module is a distinct measurement. The wording is the constant and the domain is the variable: "this area" names a different part of the firm's practice in each module, so the same words ask a genuinely different question every time. Skipping a module would not save effort already spent; it would leave a domain unread.

A second misconception is that retaking a single module breaks comparability — that a firm which reworked only its data flows must redo all one hundred questions for the results to line up. Modules can be completed in any order over time, and results update as modules complete; a fresh reading of one module simply becomes that domain's current point-in-time reading. The instrument itself is unchanged, so the new reading remains comparable with everything around it.

A third misconception treats open-ended items as filler PAT collects and ignores. Zero score weight does not mean zero purpose. The open-ended items exist to give context in the firm's own words — qualitative material that informs human judgment about what the numbers describe, while the arithmetic stays untouched by it.

A fourth misconception reads a reminder as a compliance signal — evidence that the firm is behind, flagged, or falling short of an obligation. PAT's reminders are informative nudges by design: notice that a re-assessment window has arrived, or that a module sits unfinished. They carry information about timing, never shame mechanics, and there is no compliance clock behind them.

A fifth misconception expects adaptive modules to eventually replace the core five. They do not. Adaptive modules are additional short modules — diagnostic, strength, or remediation — that can open based on a firm's scoring pattern; they extend the core assessment, while the five core modules remain the instrument that produces the five pillar results.

How many questions does a module contain? Twenty. The firm assessment has five modules, for one hundred questions in total.

Why does every module ask the same twenty questions? Identical wording is what makes the five scores comparable; the module supplies the context, so "this area" means that module's domain.

Do open-ended answers affect a module's score? No. Open-ended questions are qualitative only and carry zero score weight; scoring is deterministic arithmetic over the scaled numeric answers.

What are the three types of adaptive module? Diagnostic (probe a weak area), strength (understand why something works), and remediation (guided improvement) — all content reviewed and sourced before it can serve.

Does an AI model score modules? No. No AI model sits anywhere in the scoring path; the same answers always produce the same 0–100 result, presented band-first.
