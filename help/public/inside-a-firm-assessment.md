A PAT firm assessment is five modules of twenty questions each — one hundred questions in total — answered on a consistent numeric scale, at your own pace, one area of your practice at a time. The same twenty questions appear in every module, and that repetition is deliberate: identical wording is what makes the five resulting scores comparable. At the end, you see a score from 0 to 100 for each of PAT's five pillars, presented first as a band rather than a raw number.

## Five modules, five areas of your practice

The assessment walks through five modules, each examining a different domain of how your firm operates, and each feeding one of PAT's five pillars. Operating Model & Workflow Discipline feeds the Operations pillar. Automation & AI Readiness feeds Automation. Integration & Data Flow Maturity feeds Integration. Governance, Controls & Vendor Risk feeds Governance. Strategy, Change Readiness & Market Alignment feeds Strategy.

Together the five modules cover the ground a firm leader would want covered: how work actually gets done day to day, how ready the practice is to automate, how well its systems and data connect, how it governs itself and its vendor relationships, and how deliberately it faces its market. No single module tries to capture the whole firm; each takes one domain seriously, and the five together assemble the full picture.

## Why the same questions repeat in every module

Every module asks the same twenty questions, in identical wording, and this is by design rather than economy. When a question refers to "this area," the module you are inside supplies the meaning: in the integration module, "this area" is your integration and data-flow practice; in the governance module, the very same words point at governance. The question holds still while the context moves.

The payoff is comparability. Because each pillar's score is built from answers to identically worded questions, the five scores can be read against one another honestly. A firm that scores higher on Operations than on Automation is seeing a real difference in its own maturity, not an artifact of one module's questions being phrased more generously than another's. If each module had its own bespoke question set, a gap between two pillar scores could always be argued away as a difference in the questions. With a shared question set, the gap means what it appears to mean.

Answering the same questions five times can feel repetitive in the moment. That feeling is the cost of the design, and it is worth paying: the repetition is precisely what lets the five numbers at the end stand side by side.

## How a module is organized inside

Each of the five modules is organized into sections, so its twenty scored questions arrive as a structured walk through that module's domain rather than an undifferentiated list. The sections give the module shape: you move through the territory of a pillar in coherent steps, which makes it easier to hold the right part of your practice in mind as you answer.

Alongside the twenty scored questions, each module carries a small set of open-ended questions. These are qualitative and carry zero score weight — their job is to capture context in the firm's own words. The numeric scale records where you place your firm; the open-ended responses record how you would describe the same territory yourself, in language no scale could supply. A firm might place itself mid-scale on questions in the integration module and then, in an open-ended response, explain the specific handoff between systems that drives that placement. The score arithmetic never touches that explanation, but the explanation is part of the assessment's value: it preserves the reasoning behind the numbers, in the voice of the people who gave them.

## How you answer

Questions are answered on a consistent numeric scale, so responding is a matter of honest placement rather than essay-writing. The scale stays consistent throughout, which means you are never re-learning how to answer as you move between modules — your attention stays on your firm, not on the instrument.

Alongside the scaled questions, the assessment includes open-ended questions. These carry zero score weight. They exist to gather qualitative context — the texture behind your numeric answers — and nothing you write in them moves a score up or down. You can be as candid or as brief in them as you like, knowing they inform understanding rather than arithmetic.

Because there is no model interpreting your responses into a score — PAT's scoring is deterministic arithmetic, and the same answers always produce the same result — there is also nothing to perform for. The most useful strategy is simply accuracy. An assessment answered aspirationally produces a portrait of the firm you wish you ran; answered honestly, it produces a portrait of the firm you actually run, which is the only portrait worth having.

## Moving at your own pace

The assessment is not a timed exercise, and there is no prize for speed. You progress at your own pace, module by module, and the questions reward reflection: many will prompt you to think about how your firm genuinely behaves rather than how its procedures manual says it behaves. Some firm leaders will know their answers immediately; others will want to sit with a question, or complete a module and return for the next when they can give it proper attention.

It is worth treating the hundred questions as an occasion rather than a chore. Firms rarely make time to ask themselves the same disciplined questions about five different domains of their practice in one structured pass. The assessment's format does that work for you; your part is to bring honest answers to it. The questions themselves — before any score comes back — can surface things worth discussing inside the firm, simply because the exercise invites a domain-by-domain look that ordinary busy weeks rarely allow.

## Stopping, resuming, and completing modules in any order

The assessment is a paged flow with autosave, which means it bends to the shape of a working week rather than demanding one. You can stop in the middle of a module — mid-page, even — and resume later without losing anything. Progress is tracked per module, so at any moment you can see how far along each of the five modules stands, which are complete, and where you left off.

The five modules can also be completed in any order, over whatever span of time suits the firm. There is no prescribed sequence: a managing partner might start with Governance because a vendor review has made it top of mind, while the operations lead takes up Operating Model & Workflow Discipline the following week. Because the same twenty questions appear in every module, no ordering advantages or disadvantages any pillar — each module is self-sufficient, carrying its own context with it.

Results update as modules complete. You do not wait for question one hundred to see anything back; each finished module feeds its pillar, so the picture assembles progressively. A firm two modules in already has two pillar scores, banded and readable, while the remaining three wait for their turn. The practical effect is that the hundred questions become approachable: not one sitting of a hundred, but five sittings of twenty, taken in the order and at the pace the firm chooses.

## What you see at the end

When the assessment is complete, you see five pillar scores — Operations, Automation, Integration, Governance, Strategy — each on a 0-to-100 scale. Bands lead the presentation: each score arrives labeled as Early, Developing, Building, Established, or Leading, with the raw number in a supporting role. The band tells you at a glance what neighborhood a pillar is in; the number gives it precision.

Because all five scores were produced from identically worded questions on the same scale, they are genuinely comparable, and the shape they make together is often the most useful thing in the result. A firm might find itself Established in Operations but Developing in Integration — a pattern that says something specific about where attention would matter most, in a way no single overall grade could.

Your results also arrive with signal-integrity context. PAT runs response-pattern quality checks — for example, detecting when answers were straight-lined — so that readers of a result know how much weight it can bear. If an integrity flag accompanies a result, it is information about the response pattern, not an accusation about the respondent. It exists so that no one, including you, over-reads a score that the underlying answers cannot fully support.

## A reading at a point in time, not a permanent label

PAT treats an assessment score as a reading at a point in time, not a permanent label, and the assessment can be re-taken as operations change. A firm that reorganizes its workflows, connects two systems that used to trade spreadsheets, or overhauls its vendor governance is not the firm its last assessment described — and the instrument is built to be run again.

Band movement over time is the intended long-term reading. A firm re-assessing across quarters can see whether an area actually moved: Integration going from Developing to Building between one reading and the next says something no single assessment could — that the work done in between registered in the measurement. Bands lead that presentation just as they lead a single result, with the raw 0-to-100 numbers in support, so movement is read in the same vocabulary as position.

The cadence is deliberately humane. PAT paces engagement — quarterly rhythms suit how firm operations genuinely change — and its reminders are no-guilt by design: informative nudges about what might be worth revisiting, never shame mechanics. The assessment is meant to be a recurring discipline a firm returns to on its own terms, not a treadmill it feels bad about stepping off.

## Where the assessment's content comes from

The questions and module content a firm works through are themselves governed. Assessment and learning module content must cite authoritative sources to enter the system at all — unsourced content is rejected mechanically at import, before any human ever weighs its merits. Content that clears that gate goes live only after a named human reviewer signs off, and that review discipline is recorded: who reviewed, and when.

For a firm sitting the assessment, this governance is mostly invisible, and that is the point. The hundred questions in front of you arrived through a pipeline that refused unsourced material by machine and required an accountable person to approve what remained. An instrument that asks a firm to answer honestly owes the same discipline to its own content, and this is how PAT pays that debt.

## What the assessment is not

It is not an exam, and there is no passing mark. The five bands are a maturity vocabulary, not grades to be gamed, and a firm landing in Early or Developing on a pillar has learned something valuable, not failed something. It is not a survey of opinion, either: the questions are structured and consistently scaled precisely so the results can function as measurement rather than mood. And it is not a judgment rendered by an algorithm about your firm — the arithmetic simply adds up what you told it, and what you told it remains the substance of the result.

## Why PAT works this way

The design decision underneath the assessment's re-take model is that a score must be honest about its own shelf life. Firm operations change — systems get connected, workflows get rebuilt, governance gets overhauled — so a permanent label would be a false one. PAT therefore treats every score as a reading at a point in time and builds the instrument to be run again on a working rhythm. Re-assessment is not an admission that the first reading failed; it is the design working as intended, because the only honest way to describe a changing thing is to measure it more than once.

The zero score weight on open-ended questions follows a related principle: qualitative context should inform human judgment, not sway arithmetic. If free-text answers could move a score, two firms giving the same scaled answers could land in different places because one wrote more persuasively — and the comparability that the shared question set works so hard to establish would leak away through the text boxes. Keeping the open-ended responses at zero weight preserves both goods at once: the scores stay strictly comparable, and the firm's own words stay in the record, where a human reader can weigh them as context.

The sourcing gate on assessment content reflects the profession the instrument serves. In a field built on standards, content is only as trustworthy as its sources, so PAT enforces that standard mechanically: unsourced material is rejected at import before any human weighs its merits, and what survives goes live only after a named reviewer signs off, with the review recorded — who, and when. An instrument that asks firms for disciplined answers holds its own questions to the same discipline.

## A closer look: one scenario, start to finish

Consider an invented firm — call it Bryant & Soto, a practice starting its first PAT assessment. The managing partner opens with Governance, Controls & Vendor Risk, because a vendor review has put governance top of mind; there is no prescribed order, so starting where attention already lives costs nothing. Midway through the module, a client call interrupts — the paged flow autosaves, and the module waits, mid-page, until the following morning.

That same week, the operations lead takes up Operating Model & Workflow Discipline. Both people notice the same twenty questions appearing in their respective modules, worded identically, with "this area" meaning governance in one sitting and day-to-day workflow in the other. In the integration module a fortnight later, the partner places the firm mid-scale on several questions and then uses an open-ended response to describe the specific handoff between two systems that drives those placements — knowing the explanation carries zero score weight but will travel with the result as context.

Results assemble as modules complete: two pillars banded and readable while three still wait. When the fifth module closes, the full shape appears — say, Established in Operations, Building in Governance, Developing in Integration — with bands leading and the 0-to-100 numbers in support, and no integrity flag, because the response patterns gave the quality checks nothing to note. The pattern points attention at Integration. Two quarters later, after the firm has connected the systems that used to trade spreadsheets, Bryant & Soto re-assesses and reads the movement in the same vocabulary: Integration now Building. No single reading could have said what those two readings say together — that the work done in between registered in the measurement.

## Common misconceptions

One misreading is that repeating the same twenty questions across all five modules is padding or economy. The repetition is the instrument: identical wording is what makes the five pillar scores comparable, so a gap between two pillars reflects the firm, not the phrasing.

A second misconception is that the assessment must be completed in one sitting, or in a fixed order. It is a paged flow with autosave; progress is tracked per module, modules can be completed in any order over time, and results update as each module completes.

A third is that writing more in the open-ended questions can lift a score. Open-ended responses carry zero score weight, always. They preserve context in the firm's own words; the arithmetic never touches them.

A fourth is that a completed assessment grades the firm permanently. PAT treats every score as a reading at a point in time, and the assessment is built to be re-taken as operations change — band movement across readings is the intended long-term use.

A fifth is that an integrity flag accuses a respondent of dishonesty. It does not: response-pattern quality checks such as straight-lining detection produce information about how much weight a result can bear, not accusations about the people behind it.
