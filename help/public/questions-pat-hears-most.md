People considering PAT tend to ask the same questions, and this article answers them in one place. PAT — Performance Alignment Technology, a Patalign™ product — measures how well the accounting ecosystem's tools and practices actually line up: firms assess their own operations, vendors declare what their products do, and the platform measures the gap between the two. Every answer below is short by design; each points to a fuller public article where the topic gets the room it deserves.

## What is PAT, and who is it for?

PAT is Performance Alignment Technology, a Patalign™ product built for the accounting ecosystem. It serves three roles. Accounting firms use it to assess their own operations across five pillars. Software vendors who serve those firms use it to declare their products' capabilities and receive evidence of how those capabilities land in practice. Consultants and ecosystem owners who manage sets of firms use it to see the alignment picture across the whole group they look after. Each role sees what its role permits, and nothing more. The full front-door answer lives in "What is PAT," and each role has a dedicated article: "PAT for firms," "PAT for vendors," and "PAT for consultants and ecosystem owners."

## What does a firm assessment actually involve?

A firm assessment is five modules of 20 questions each — 100 questions in total — answered on a consistent numeric scale. The five modules map to PAT's five pillars: Operating Model & Workflow Discipline maps to Operations, Automation & AI Readiness to Automation, Integration & Data Flow Maturity to Integration, Governance, Controls & Vendor Risk to Governance, and Strategy, Change Readiness & Market Alignment to Strategy. The same 20 questions repeat in every module on purpose: identical wording is what makes the five scores comparable, while the module supplies the context — "this area" means that module's domain. Open-ended questions carry zero score weight; they are qualitative only. "Inside a firm assessment" walks through the journey, and "The five pillars, briefly" tours what each pillar looks at.

## How is a PAT score calculated — and does AI generate it?

PAT scores come from deterministic arithmetic on a 0–100 scale: the same answers always produce the same score, and no AI model sits anywhere in the scoring path. Results present through five bands — Early, Developing, Building, Established, Leading — and the bands lead the presentation, with the raw numbers in support. PAT does include an assistant, Ask Pat, but it helps people understand documented content; it never generates scores. If the mechanics interest you, "How PAT scoring works" explains the design, and "Why PAT scores are not AI-generated" sets out the determinism position and exactly where assistance does and does not sit.

## What do the five bands mean?

Bands are the five-level vocabulary PAT scores speak in: Early, Developing, Building, Established, and Leading. The band leads every presentation, with the raw 0–100 number in support, because a band communicates position more honestly than a bare number invites over-reading. Band movement over time is the intended long-term reading: a firm re-assessing across quarters can see whether an area moved — say, from Developing to Building — which tells a more useful story than any single snapshot. "From Early to Leading: the bands" walks through the whole lexicon and how to read it.

## Do I have to finish an assessment in one sitting?

No. The assessment experience is a paged flow with autosave: you can stop and resume, progress is tracked per module, and the five modules can be completed in any order over time, with results updating as each module completes. Assessments can also be re-taken as operations change, because PAT treats a score as a reading at a point in time rather than a permanent label — and its reminders are deliberately no-guilt nudges, never shame mechanics. "Inside a firm assessment" describes the experience end to end.

## What is the alignment delta?

The alignment delta is the measured gap between what a vendor declares a product can do and what firms, through their assessments, experience it doing. It flows both ways: a product can outperform its declaration just as it can underperform it, so the delta is useful information for vendors and firms alike rather than a verdict on either. It is the concept PAT is named for — performance alignment is what the delta measures. "The alignment delta, introduced" gives the concept a full orientation, and "What vendors declare and firms verify" explains the two-sided model that produces it.

## What are PAT benchmarks, and why do some show as suppressed?

PAT benchmarks are comparison context, never rankings or league tables, and contributors are anonymous within them. A benchmark cut publishes only when its cohort has at least 5 contributors and no single contributor supplies more than 25% of the data; when a cut fails either test, it shows as suppressed rather than publishing a number that could mislead or expose someone. Suppression is not a malfunction — it is the integrity rule doing its job. "How PAT protects benchmark integrity" explains the contributor floor and dominance cap in plain terms.

## Are benchmarks a ranking of firms or vendors?

No. PAT benchmarks are comparison context, never rankings or league tables, and contributors are anonymous within them — no leaderboard exists anywhere in the platform. A benchmark tells you what a qualifying cohort looks like so you can place your own reading in context; it never places named participants above one another. "What PAT benchmarks are — and are never" draws that line precisely, including what benchmarks are for when they are not for competing.

## Does demo or pilot data ever end up in real benchmarks?

No. PAT keeps production, pilot, and demo data strictly separated, and demo, synthetic, and pilot data never enter published benchmarks. Benchmarks are built from production data only, so the comparison context a member sees reflects real assessed operations rather than trial runs or sample content. The full explanation of these boundaries is in "Production, pilot, and demo data never mix," and the broader trust posture — role walls, tenancy isolation, permission-scoped assistance — is described in "Security and privacy posture."

## What does "evidence, not advertising" mean?

"Evidence, not advertising" is PAT's philosophy for how claims get treated. Vendors make structured declarations rather than free-form marketing statements; firm assessments verify how those declarations hold up in practice; and every displayed number traces back to its sources — PAT calls this evidence lineage, and the working rule is that there are no orphan numbers. Aspiration alone does not score. The article "Evidence, not advertising" unpacks why the platform is built this way.

## Is there a free tier?

No, and that is a design decision rather than an omission. PAT offers paid membership at two tiers, Pro and Elite, because benchmark quality depends on committed participants: comparison context is only worth having when the people contributing to it are serious about the data they contribute. Elite adds deeper interpretive surfaces, such as where a result sits within a distribution and how it trends over time. "Why PAT has no free tier" explains the membership philosophy in full.

## What happens to our data if we leave?

Your data belongs to you — that is PAT's stewardship position for every tenant. If a tenant leaves the platform, their data is removed, and the removal is receipted rather than simply asserted. The only thing that persists is what was always anonymous: benchmark aggregates that already passed the suppression rules remain aggregate, and individual contributions cannot be reconstructed from them. "Security and privacy posture" covers this stewardship position alongside the platform's role walls and permission scoping.

## What is Ask Pat, and what can it see?

Ask Pat is PAT's assistant. It answers questions using PAT's own documented content only, and it is role-scoped — each user sees only content for their role, enforced at the data layer, not just at the interface. When Ask Pat lacks documented evidence for an answer, it says so rather than guessing. It assists with understanding what is in front of you; it never generates scores. "Why PAT scores are not AI-generated" covers where Ask Pat sits relative to scoring, and "Security and privacy posture" covers how its role-scoping works at trust-page level.

## What is an ecosystem in PAT?

An ecosystem is a consultant-managed construct: a set of member firms plus the products in play across them. The consultant or ecosystem owner sees the cross-firm alignment structure — how the group's operations and tool stacks line up — while firms and vendors within the ecosystem each see only what their role permits. It is how someone responsible for many firms gets a structured signal across all of them without any firm's view leaking into another's. "What an ecosystem is in PAT" describes the construct, and "PAT for consultants and ecosystem owners" describes the role that manages it.

## What happens when I first sign in?

Your first session is orientation, not a test. A new firm begins with the five-module assessment at its own pace; a new vendor begins by making structured declarations about its products. Nothing about the experience depends on getting through it quickly, and the consistent question design means you always know what kind of answer is being asked of you. "Your first session with PAT" describes what day one looks like for each role, and "Inside a firm assessment" goes deeper on the assessment journey itself.

## Do open-ended questions count toward my score?

No. Each module carries a small set of open-ended questions alongside its 20 scored ones, and they carry zero score weight by design — they are qualitative only, there to capture context in the firm's own words. Keeping them out of the arithmetic is what keeps scores comparable across firms while still preserving what a firm wanted to say in its own voice. Scores rest entirely on the numerically scaled responses. "How PAT scoring works" explains what does and does not enter the arithmetic, and "Inside a firm assessment" shows where the open-ended questions sit in the journey.

## What is a signal-integrity flag, and should I worry about one?

A signal-integrity flag is the output of response-pattern quality checks — straight-lining detection is one example — that accompany scores so readers know how much weight a result can bear. It is information, not an accusation: a flag calibrates how a reading should be weighed rather than declaring anyone's answers dishonest. Flags travel with the results they describe, so nothing in PAT presents a number while hiding what it rests on. "How PAT scoring works" covers where these checks sit relative to the scoring arithmetic.

## What do the Pro and Elite tiers each cover?

At concept level, Pro covers the core of membership: assessment, scores, bands, and core insights. Elite adds deeper interpretive surfaces — where a firm sits within a distribution of peers when benchmarks publish under the suppression rules, how scores are trending across assessments, and category-level context. The tiers differ in interpretive depth, not in the integrity of the underlying measurement, which is identical for everyone. PAT does not publish prices in its articles. "Why PAT has no free tier" explains the membership philosophy the two tiers sit inside.

## Where does the content inside PAT come from?

Every piece of learning and assessment content passes a governance discipline before it can serve. Content must cite authoritative sources to enter the system at all — unsourced material is rejected mechanically at import — and what passes that gate goes live only after named human review sign-off, with the review recorded: who reviewed it, and when. So the questions a firm answers and the documentation Ask Pat draws on carry a recorded chain behind them. "Evidence, not advertising" describes the philosophy this discipline serves.

## Can a consultant read our firm's answers?

Consultants see structure and alignment signal across the ecosystem they manage — per-firm cards summarizing alignment status, structured firm briefs, and cross-ecosystem comparison views — but not a firm's private answer text beyond what the role permits. The boundary is enforced at the data layer, not just at the interface, so it holds regardless of how a question is asked. "PAT for consultants and ecosystem owners" describes what the consultant role sees, and "Security and privacy posture" covers how role walls are enforced.

## Are there modules beyond the core five?

At concept level, yes: additional short modules can open based on a firm's scoring pattern. A diagnostic module probes a weak area, a strength module helps a firm understand why something works, and a remediation module offers guided improvement. Like all PAT content, these serve only after their material has been sourced and reviewed. The core assessment remains the five-module, 100-question structure. "Inside a firm assessment" covers the assessment journey these modules can extend.

## Who built PAT?

PAT was built inside the profession it serves — by people who run and advise practices — rather than adapted from a generic survey tool. That origin shows up in the design more than in any origin story: the pillar structure, the insistence that every displayed number trace to its sources, and the treatment of scores as measurements rather than marketing all reflect it. "What is PAT" gives the full front-door picture of what that design produced.

## How does PAT pace engagement over time?

Deliberately, and without guilt. PAT paces engagement around working rhythms such as quarterly ones, and its reminders are informative nudges about where things stand — never shame mechanics about what is unfinished. The long-term reading the platform is built for is band movement across re-assessments: whether an area moved, say, from Developing to Building, matters more than any single snapshot.
