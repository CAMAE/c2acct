PAT scores are produced by deterministic arithmetic from a firm's answers to a structured assessment: five modules, one hundred questions in total, each answered on a consistent numeric scale, rolled up into scores from 0 to 100 and presented in five named bands. There is no judgment call in the calculation and no variability in the math — the same answers always produce the same score. This article explains the mechanics at orientation level, and why PAT was designed this way.

## A structured assessment: five modules, one hundred questions

A PAT firm assessment consists of five modules with twenty questions each — one hundred questions in total. Each module maps to one of PAT's five pillars: Operating Model & Workflow Discipline maps to Operations, Automation & AI Readiness to Automation, Integration & Data Flow Maturity to Integration, Governance, Controls & Vendor Risk to Governance, and Strategy, Change Readiness & Market Alignment to Strategy.

Questions are answered on a consistent numeric scale, which is the first design decision that makes scoring possible at all. When every answer lands on the same scale, answers can be combined arithmetically, compared across questions, and rolled up into a module score without any interpretive step in between. The assessment also includes open-ended questions, and these carry zero score weight — they are qualitative only. What you write in an open-ended response enriches the picture, but it never moves a number.

## Why the same twenty questions repeat in every module

The twenty questions in each module are the same twenty questions, repeated across all five modules — and this is deliberate, not an economy. Identical wording is what makes the five pillar scores comparable. When a firm scores differently on Operations than on Integration, that difference is real, because both scores came from the same questions answered in different contexts. The module supplies the context: "this area" means that module's domain, so the same question does honest work five times over.

If each module asked different questions, a gap between two pillar scores could always be an artifact of the questions rather than a fact about the firm — one module's questions might simply be harder, or worded more generously. Repeating the questions removes that ambiguity. The instrument stays constant; only the domain under examination changes. It can feel unusual the first time you encounter it, which is exactly why it is worth saying plainly: the repetition is the mechanism of comparability.

## From answers to a 0–100 score

Scoring in PAT is deterministic arithmetic on a 0–100 scale. Your scaled answers are combined by fixed calculation into module scores — nothing more exotic than that. Deterministic means exactly what it sounds like: given the same set of answers, the calculation produces the same score, every time, for every firm. There is no model interpreting your responses, no weighting that shifts by mood or moment, and no step where a number appears that cannot be traced back to the answers that produced it.

That traceability reflects a broader PAT principle: displayed numbers trace to their sources. A score is not an opinion about your firm; it is an arithmetic consequence of what your firm said, and the path from answers to score can always be followed. PAT calls this evidence lineage — no orphan numbers.

The zero weight on open-ended questions serves the same principle from the other side. Qualitative responses are valuable, but they cannot be added, averaged, or scaled without someone — or something — interpreting them first, and any interpretive step would break the clean line from answers to number. So the arithmetic runs only on the scaled answers, and written responses stay what they are: context, kept fully out of the calculation.

## Inside the instrument: sections and open-ended context

The five-module, 100-question structured assessment has a little more internal shape than the headline numbers suggest. Modules contain sections, which organize the twenty scored questions into a workable flow rather than a single undifferentiated list. Alongside the scored questions, each module carries a small set of open-ended questions — qualitative items that let a firm give context in its own words.

The division of labor between the two kinds of question is strict. The twenty scored questions in each module, answered on the consistent numeric scale, are the entire input to that module's arithmetic. The open-ended questions carry zero score weight, always: they exist so that the numbers are accompanied by the firm's own account of its situation, and they never touch the calculation. A firm that writes three thoughtful paragraphs and a firm that writes nothing will score identically if their scaled answers are identical. That is not a limitation of the design — it is the design. The score stays a pure arithmetic consequence of the scaled answers, and the written context stays what it is: meaning around the number, not an ingredient in it.

## When scores compute: module by module

A module's score is arithmetic on that module's twenty scaled answers, which means each module can score as soon as its answers are in. Results update as modules complete: a firm that has finished two of the five modules has two real pillar scores, computed by exactly the same arithmetic that will compute the other three. Modules can be completed in any order over time, so the five-pillar picture fills in progressively rather than appearing all at once at the end.

Nothing about a score changes when the later modules arrive. The Operations score computed after one module is the same Operations score that appears after all five, because it depends only on the Operations answers — deterministic arithmetic has no view of what else has or has not been completed. What completing more modules adds is breadth, not revision: more of the five-pillar picture, each part standing on its own answers.

## A worked reading: two pillars, one firm

An illustration, with invented numbers, shows how the pieces read together. Suppose a firm completes its assessment and scores 74 on Operations and 62 on Integration. Both numbers came from the same twenty questions, answered on the same scale, scored by the same arithmetic — only the module context differed. So the twelve-point difference is a fact about the firm, not an artifact of the instrument: this firm assesses its operating discipline more strongly than its integration and data-flow maturity, by its own structured answers.

Each score presents band-first — the band names the firm's position in that area, and the raw number sits in support. Read that way, the headline is not "74 versus 62" but which band each area occupies, with the numbers available when finer detail is genuinely useful. Now suppose the same firm's Governance responses show a straight-line pattern — the same answer selected down page after page. The Governance score still computes; arithmetic is arithmetic. But the integrity signal accompanying it notes the pattern, so anyone reading that score knows to stand on it more lightly than on the Operations and Integration results. Three scores, one instrument, and an honest account of how much weight each reading can bear — that is the whole scoring system in miniature.

The example also shows evidence lineage doing its quiet work. If anyone at this firm asks where the 74 came from, the answer is complete and concrete: from the firm's twenty scaled Operations answers, combined by fixed arithmetic — nothing else contributed, and nothing about the number needs interpreting before it can be explained. Every score in the illustration can give the same account of itself, which is what PAT means when it says no number is an orphan.

## A score is a reading, not a label

PAT treats a score as a reading at a point in time, not a permanent label. Operations change — firms adopt new tools, tighten workflows, restructure — and assessments can be re-taken as they do. A firm that scored 62 on Integration and then spent two quarters overhauling its data flows can assess again and see where the new answers land.

Deterministic scoring is what makes that second reading meaningful. Because the same answers always produce the same score, a changed score can only mean changed answers — the instrument cannot drift underneath you between readings. The comparison between this quarter's result and last quarter's is therefore a comparison of the firm at two moments, measured identically at both. A scoring system with any variability in the math could never support that claim; PAT's can, and that is much of why the arithmetic is built the way it is.

## Bands lead, numbers support

Results present in five bands: Early, Developing, Building, Established, and Leading. In every PAT surface, the band leads the presentation and the raw number supports it.

The reasoning is about honest precision. A 0–100 scale invites over-reading — treating a two-point difference as meaningful when it may simply be noise in how two people answered a handful of questions. Bands communicate position at the resolution the instrument actually supports. A firm in Building is in a different place than a firm in Established, and that statement is sturdier than a comparison of, say, 61 versus 63. The raw number is still there, still exact, and still useful — it simply plays a supporting role rather than the headline.

## Integrity signals travel with scores

Every score is accompanied by signal-integrity information: response-pattern quality checks, such as straight-lining detection, that tell readers how much weight a result can bear. If a response pattern suggests, for example, that someone selected the same answer down an entire page, the score still computes — arithmetic is arithmetic — but the integrity signal notes the pattern so the number is read with appropriate caution.

An integrity flag is information, not an accusation. It does not claim anyone answered in bad faith; it simply reports what the response pattern looks like, so a score is never presented as more load-bearing than its underlying answers justify. Scores tell you where the answers landed; integrity signals tell you how firmly to stand on them.

## Why PAT scoring is designed this way

Every design choice in PAT scoring serves the same end: scores you can compare and trust. Comparability is why the questions repeat and why the answer scale is consistent — two firms' Integration scores, or one firm's Operations and Governance scores, mean the same thing measured the same way. Trust is why the arithmetic is deterministic and traceable — a score that cannot change without the answers changing is a score a firm, a vendor, or a consultant can build a conversation on. Honesty about resolution is why bands lead — the presentation matches what the instrument can genuinely distinguish. And humility about inputs is why integrity signals travel with every result — good arithmetic on weak answers is still weak evidence, and PAT says so.

This design also carries weight beyond any single firm. Firm scores contribute to the comparison context PAT offers, and that context is only as good as the consistency of what feeds it. A deterministic, uniformly structured instrument means every contribution is made with the same measuring stick — which is precisely what makes comparison context worth having.

## What a score is, and what it is not

It is worth closing with the boundaries. A PAT score is a structured, repeatable, arithmetic summary of how a firm answered a consistent set of questions about an area of its operations. It is not a rating handed down by an algorithm's judgment, not a ranking against other firms, and not a verdict on quality — it is a position, expressed in a band, supported by a number, accompanied by an honest reading of how much weight it can bear.

## Why PAT works this way

Two commitments in the design deserve stating in their sharpest form, because everything else follows from them. The first: a measurement instrument must stay constant so that only the lens changes. If question wording varied from pillar to pillar, a difference between two pillar scores could always be a wording artifact, and no amount of careful arithmetic downstream could repair that. So PAT changes the lens, never the instrument: the same twenty question stems, the same scale, five domains under examination.

The second: measurements must be repeatable and auditable. Model behavior can shift over time, and a score that could read the same answers differently on different days is an opinion with a timestamp, not a measurement. Deterministic arithmetic is the only scoring path that lets PAT stand behind every comparison it invites — across pillars, across firms, across quarters — because it is the only path on which nothing but the answers can move the result.

The zero weight on open-ended questions completes the pattern. Qualitative context should inform human judgment, not sway arithmetic. A firm's own words are kept — and kept out of the calculation — so scores stay comparable while the account behind them stays human. Three rules, one instinct: protect the meaning of the number by controlling exactly what is allowed to produce it.

## How this looks for a firm, a vendor, and a consultant

For a firm, the scoring design is experienced as a journey that respects its time: a paged flow with autosave, progress tracked per module, modules completed in any order, and real pillar scores appearing as each module finishes rather than after a long wait. For a vendor, the same design is experienced secondhand but critically: the alignment evidence a vendor receives — how firms' assessed experience meets its declarations — rests on firm scores, and that evidence is worth exactly what the scoring discipline behind those scores is worth. For a consultant managing an ecosystem, the design is what makes a portfolio readable: every member firm's results come from the same instrument and the same arithmetic, so differences between firms on a comparison view are differences between firms. One scoring system, three reasons to care that it holds still.

## A closer look: one scenario, start to finish

Take an invented firm that completes its assessment and scores 74 on Operations and 62 on Integration, and follow it further. The firm decides its integration and data-flow maturity deserves the next two quarters' attention. The open-ended responses it wrote — a few paragraphs describing, in its own words, where data gets re-keyed between tools — moved no numbers, but they give the partners a concrete account to work from alongside the score.

Two quarters later, after overhauling how data moves between its practice tools, the firm re-assesses. The Integration module asks the same twenty questions, on the same scale, scored by the same arithmetic — the instrument has not moved. Suppose the new answers land at 71, and the result now presents in Building where it previously presented in Developing — invented numbers and bands, purely illustrative. Because nothing in the scoring path changed between the two readings, the nine-point movement has exactly one available explanation: the firm's answers changed. The Operations score, re-taken in the same round, lands near where it did before — which is itself information, confirming that a stable area reads stably. And the whole trajectory — 62 to 71, one band to the next — can be explained to anyone in the firm without a single interpretive step: these answers, this arithmetic, this result, both times.

## Common misconceptions

*Repeating the same twenty questions across modules is a shortcut.* It is the mechanism of comparability. Identical wording is what guarantees that a difference between two pillar scores reflects the firm rather than the phrasing; varied questions would reintroduce exactly the ambiguity the design removes.

*Writing rich open-ended answers improves a score.* Open-ended questions carry zero score weight, always. Two firms with identical scaled answers score identically regardless of what either wrote. Written responses provide context in the firm's own words; they never touch the arithmetic.

*A small difference in raw scores is a meaningful difference.* PAT leads with bands precisely because a 0–100 scale invites false precision. A two-point gap between raw numbers may be noise in how two people answered a handful of questions; a band difference is a statement the instrument can support.

*Scores are adjusted for firm size, type, or anything else about who is answering.* The arithmetic runs on scaled answers and nothing else. There is no weighting by profile and no normalization by identity — a score is an arithmetic consequence of answers alone.

*No scores exist until the whole assessment is finished.* Each module's score computes from that module's twenty answers as soon as they are in. Results update as modules complete, and a finished module's score does not change when later modules arrive.

## Questions this article answers

**How is a PAT score calculated?** By deterministic arithmetic on a firm's scaled answers — twenty scored questions per module, rolled up to a 0–100 score and presented band-first.

**How many questions does a firm assessment contain?** One hundred scored questions — five modules of twenty each — plus a small set of open-ended questions that carry no score weight.

**Why does every module use the same twenty questions?** Identical wording keeps the five pillar scores comparable; the module supplies the context, so only the domain changes, never the instrument.

**Do open-ended answers affect the score?** No — they are qualitative only and carry zero score weight, so the score depends entirely on the scaled answers.

**When do scores appear?** Module by module: each pillar score computes as soon as its module's answers are complete, in whatever order the modules are taken.

**Why do bands lead the presentation instead of raw numbers?** Bands communicate position at the resolution the measurement honestly supports; the raw number stays available in support.

**What happens to a score if the responses show straight-lining?** The score still computes, but a signal-integrity note accompanies it so readers know how much weight the result can bear.
