PAT assesses accounting firms across five pillars — Operations, Automation, Integration, Governance, and Strategy — each measured by its own assessment module and each scored on the same 0–100 scale. There are five because a firm's health is not one number: a practice can run disciplined workflows while its systems barely talk to each other, or adopt automation enthusiastically while its controls lag behind. Five comparable scores show the shape of a firm, not just its size.

## Why five pillars instead of one score

A single blended score would be easy to read and almost useless to act on. Two firms with the same overall number can be in completely different situations: one strong on process and weak on technology, the other the reverse. Averaging those differences away hides exactly the information a managing partner, a vendor, or a consultant needs.

So PAT keeps the dimensions separate. Each pillar corresponds to one assessment module, each module carries twenty questions, and each pillar receives its own score from 0 to 100, presented first as one of five bands — Early, Developing, Building, Established, Leading — with the raw number in support. The five dimensions were chosen to cover the things that determine whether a firm and its technology are pulling in the same direction: how work runs, what is automated, how data moves, how risk is controlled, and where the firm is heading.

One design decision makes the five scores genuinely comparable rather than five separate surveys stapled together: every module asks the same twenty questions, with identical wording, on purpose. The module supplies the context — when a question refers to "this area," that means the module's own domain. Because the wording never shifts, a 70 on one pillar means the same kind of thing as a 70 on another, and the differences between your five scores are real differences, not artifacts of how questions were phrased.

## The twenty questions, and the questions that never score

Each of PAT's five assessment modules covers its pillar through the same twenty scored questions, answered on a consistent numeric scale and organized into sections within the module. Those twenty answers are the entire numeric input to the pillar's score: the arithmetic is deterministic, so the same answers always produce the same 0–100 result, and no AI model sits anywhere in the scoring path.

Alongside the scored questions, every module also carries a small set of open-ended questions. These are qualitative by design and carry zero score weight — nothing written in them moves a number anywhere. Their job is context: they let a firm describe, in its own words, what sits behind the answers it has just given, so each pillar's picture is not only a band and a score but also the firm's own account of its situation.

That separation is deliberate. Because open-ended answers never touch the score, there is no incentive to write toward a result, and the numeric side of every pillar stays clean and comparable across all five. The words add texture; the twenty scored questions carry all of the weight.

## Operations: how work actually moves through the firm

The Operations pillar is measured by the module called Operating Model & Workflow Discipline. This is the pillar about the day-to-day reality of the practice: the way the firm has defined how work should run, and how consistently work actually runs that way.

Every firm has an operating model, whether or not it is written down. What varies enormously is discipline — whether engagements follow a repeatable path or depend on whichever person happens to handle them, whether handoffs are designed or improvised. When you answer this module's questions, "this area" means exactly that territory: your operating model and the discipline of your workflows.

Operations earns its place as the first pillar because it is the foundation the other four stand on. Automation amplifies whatever process it is pointed at; integration connects the systems those processes run through. A firm that understands its Operations score understands the ground it is building everything else upon.

## Automation: what you automate and how ready you are

The Automation pillar is measured by the module called Automation & AI Readiness. It looks at the degree to which the firm has automated its work and — just as importantly — how ready the firm is to adopt automation and AI where it has not yet done so.

Readiness is the operative word. This pillar is not a checklist of tools owned; it is a picture of the firm's relationship with automation: where it stands today and how prepared it is for what it takes on next. Two firms can own similar software and sit in very different bands here, because the pillar reflects the firm, not the shopping list.

One clarification worth making, since this pillar has "AI" in its module name: PAT measures your AI readiness, but no AI measures you. Scoring across all five pillars is deterministic arithmetic — the same answers always produce the same score — and no AI model sits anywhere in the scoring path.

## Integration: how data flows between your systems

The Integration pillar is measured by the module called Integration & Data Flow Maturity. It looks at how mature the firm's data flows are — how well the systems the firm relies on actually connect, and how data moves between them.

Most accounting firms run on more than one system, and the seams between systems are where friction tends to live: the same data entered more than once, information that exists in one tool but never reaches another, workarounds that quietly become permanent. Integration maturity is the difference between a collection of software and a working stack.

This pillar tends to be revealing precisely because it is hard to see from inside. Day to day, people adapt to poor data flow until it feels normal. A structured score gives the firm a way to look at that maturity directly, in the same banded terms as everything else it measures.

## Governance: controls and vendor risk

The Governance pillar is measured by the module called Governance, Controls & Vendor Risk. It looks at how the firm governs its operations and technology: the controls it maintains, and how it manages the risk that comes with depending on vendors.

For an accounting firm, this pillar covers familiar ground — the profession runs on control and trust — but points the lens inward, at the firm's own house rather than its clients'. Every tool a firm adopts is also a dependency, and governance is what determines whether those dependencies are managed or merely accumulated.

Governance often moves at a different pace from Automation and Integration, and that is exactly why it has its own pillar. Seeing an Established band in Automation next to a Developing band in Governance tells a firm something a blended score never could. To be clear about what the score is: it is a structured self-assessment of the firm's governance practices, not an audit, a certification, or compliance advice.

## Strategy: change readiness and market alignment

The Strategy pillar is measured by the module called Strategy, Change Readiness & Market Alignment. It looks forward where the other four pillars look at the present: the firm's strategic direction, its readiness to change, and how well it is aligned with the market it serves.

Change readiness sits at the center of this pillar. Firms rarely fail to see change coming; they differ in whether they are built to respond to it. Market alignment is the outward-facing half of the same question — whether where the firm is heading matches where its clients and its market actually are.

Strategy is the pillar that turns the other four from a snapshot into a direction. Operations, Automation, Integration, and Governance describe the firm as it is; Strategy describes how deliberately the firm is steering what it becomes.

## A worked illustration: one firm, five bands

Imagine an illustrative firm reading its PAT results for the first time. Its five pillars come back as Established in Operations, Established in Governance, Building in Automation, Building in Strategy, and Developing in Integration.

Read side by side, those five bands describe a recognizable practice. Work runs through disciplined, repeatable paths, and the firm's controls and vendor relationships are actively managed — the two Established bands. Automation and strategic direction are progressing but not yet mature. And the firm's systems talk to each other less well than everything else it does — Integration, at Developing, visibly trails the rest.

Because every module asked the same twenty questions in identical wording, the firm can trust that this spread reflects its operations rather than the instrument: Integration did not come back lower because that module was somehow harder. The profile also suggests an order of work. With disciplined operations and managed governance underneath, improving data flow between systems would land on stable ground — a very different starting position from attacking all five areas at once.

Now imagine a second illustrative firm whose raw scores happen to average out to the same overall level, but whose bands include Leading in Automation and Early in Governance. Same average, opposite situation: adoption running ahead of control. That contrast is precisely why PAT never blends the five pillars into one number — the shape is the information.

## A pillar score is a reading, not a label

PAT treats every pillar score as a reading taken at a point in time, not a permanent label on the firm. Operations change — firms adopt tools, redesign workflows, tighten controls — and the assessment is built to be re-taken as they do.

The long-term reading of the pillars is movement. A firm that re-assesses across quarters can see whether an area actually shifted — a pillar that showed Developing at one reading and Building at the next has a story to tell, in the same banded vocabulary as the original result. Bands lead every presentation, with the raw 0–100 numbers in support, so change over time reads in plain terms rather than in decimal noise.

The cadence around this is deliberately calm. PAT paces engagement in steady rhythms — quarterly is a natural one — and its reminders are informative nudges rather than pressure: no-guilt by design. A pillar score is there to be revisited when the firm's operations have genuinely moved, not to be chased.

## Five pillars shaped by the profession they measure

The five pillars were not adapted from a generic maturity survey. PAT was built inside the profession it serves — by people who run and advise accounting practices — and the pillar structure reflects that origin: Operations, Automation, Integration, Governance, and Strategy are the dimensions along which an accounting firm and its technology either pull together or pull apart.

That is also why PAT confines itself to one ecosystem — accounting firms, the software vendors who serve them, and the consultants and advisors who manage firm ecosystems — rather than stretching one instrument across every industry. Every firm assessed answers the same five modules; every vendor's alignment evidence is expressed against the same five pillars; every consultant reads the same five-part vocabulary across a portfolio. A pillar score carries more meaning when everyone reading it works in the same profession and is measured against the same structure.

## Five scores, read side by side

The payoff of the five-pillar design is the moment you see all five bands next to each other. The pattern is the insight: which pillars lead, which lag, and how wide the spread is. A firm whose five scores cluster tightly is in a different position from a firm with one Leading pillar and one Early pillar, even if their averages happen to match — and because every module asks the same questions in the same words, that spread reflects the firm, not the instrument.

The same five-pillar structure serves everyone in the PAT ecosystem. Firms see their own shape across the five dimensions. Vendors receive alignment evidence expressed against the same structure firms are assessed on. Consultants who manage an ecosystem of firms see the alignment picture across their portfolio in one consistent vocabulary.

## Why PAT works this way

The identical questions across all five modules exist because a measurement instrument must stay constant so that only the lens changes. If each module phrased its questions differently, a gap between two pillar scores could be a wording artifact — one module's phrasing landing softer or harder than another's — rather than a real difference in the firm. By holding the twenty questions fixed and letting the module supply the domain, PAT ensures the spread across your five scores is information about your practice, never about the questionnaire. The rule underneath is simple: change the lens, never the instrument.

The open-ended questions carry zero score weight because qualitative context should inform human judgment, not sway arithmetic. The moment written answers could move a number, the numeric side would stop being comparable across firms and pillars. Keeping words and weights separate preserves both: scores stay clean and comparable, and the firm's own account stays honest.

And bands lead the presentation because a single-point score invites false precision. A 0–100 number looks exact; what the measurement can honestly support is a band, with the raw number behind it. Movement between bands over time is the reading that matters.

## How the pillars look for a firm, a vendor, and a consultant

For a firm, the pillars are lived one module at a time. The assessment runs as a paged flow with autosave, so a firm can stop and resume; modules can be completed in any order over time, progress is tracked per module, and results update as modules complete.

For a vendor, the pillars are the vocabulary its alignment evidence is expressed in. Vendors declare product capabilities across defined function areas of practice software, and firms' assessed experience is measured against those declarations — evidence delivered against the same five-pillar structure every firm is assessed on, usable in honest sales and product conversations.

For a consultant or ecosystem owner, the pillars are a portfolio vocabulary. Consultant surfaces — per-firm cards summarizing alignment status, structured firm briefs for deeper review, and cross-ecosystem comparison views — speak in the same five pillars, so patterns across managed firms are visible without exposing any firm's private answer text beyond what the role permits.

## A closer look: one scenario, start to finish

Return to the illustrative firm whose first reading came back Established in Operations and Governance, Building in Automation and Strategy, and Developing in Integration — and give the story its full arc.

The reading itself was not a single sitting. The firm worked through the paged, autosaving flow in the module order that suited it, over several weeks, watching results update as each module finished. When the fifth module closed, the five bands stood side by side for the first time, and the shape was unmistakable: strong foundations, a progressing middle, and Integration visibly trailing.

The firm chose to work on data flow first, reasoning that disciplined operations and managed governance gave the improvement stable ground to land on. Two quarters later — on the steady, no-guilt rhythm PAT paces engagement around — the firm re-assessed, and Integration moved from Developing to Building. Because bands lead and numbers support, the movement read in plain terms, in the same vocabulary as the original reading.

At concept level, a pattern like this is also what adaptive modules exist for: a short diagnostic module can open to probe a weak area such as that early Integration result — all such content reviewed and sourced before it can serve.

## Common misconceptions about the five pillars

"The five scores roll up into one overall PAT score." They do not, and the omission is deliberate. Averaging the pillars would hide exactly the information the structure exists to show — two firms with identical averages can be in opposite situations. The shape is the information; there is no blended number.

"Since every module asks the same twenty questions, you answer them once and the answers carry over." Each module is answered separately. The wording repeats; the context does not — "this area" means a different pillar's domain in each module.

"Some modules must be harder than others, so a low pillar just reflects a tougher questionnaire." The identical wording removes that possibility. No module is phrased more harshly than another, so a lower pillar is telling you about that area of the firm, not the instrument.

"The open-ended questions quietly influence the score." They carry zero score weight by design; the twenty scored questions per module are the entire numeric input.
