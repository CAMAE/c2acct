A PAT benchmark is comparison context: a way for a firm or a vendor to see their own result against a wider backdrop of results, so a score means something beyond itself. It is never a ranking, a league table, or a public scoreboard — those things do not exist anywhere in PAT, by design rather than by omission. Contributors to a benchmark stay anonymous inside it, and when a comparison group is too small or too lopsided to be trustworthy, PAT shows nothing at all rather than something misleading.

## What a benchmark is for

A score on its own answers one question: how did this assessment come out? It cannot answer the question most readers actually bring to it: is that good? A firm that completes PAT's five-pillar assessment and sees its Integration result naturally wants to know whether that result is unusual, typical, or notable — and a number with no surroundings cannot say. Benchmarks exist to supply the surroundings. They place a result in the context of comparable results, so that "here is your score" can become "here is your score, and here is the landscape it sits in."

That is the entire job. A PAT benchmark is context for interpretation, not a verdict, not a grade posted on a wall, and not an instrument for pressuring anyone. The distinction matters because comparison data is only honest when the people contributing to it are not being set up for exposure. PAT's benchmarks are built so that contributing to the picture never means becoming a target inside it.

## Never a ranking, never a league table

PAT does not rank firms. It does not rank vendors or their products. There is no leaderboard, no ordered list of who came first and who came last, and no "top firms" feature waiting behind any tier or role. This is a deliberate position, not a missing feature.

Rankings change what measurement is for. The moment results become an ordered public list, the incentive shifts from answering honestly to placing well, and the data degrades into performance for an audience. PAT's assessment depends on firms describing their operations as they actually are — that is what makes the resulting scores worth comparing at all. Comparison as context preserves that honesty: a firm can see where its result sits within a wider picture without anyone else being able to point at its position. Comparison as ranking would spend that honesty to make a spectacle.

So when PAT says benchmarks are "context, never rankings," it is describing a boundary the product is built around. You can learn where you stand. Nobody gets to publish where you stand.

## Anonymity inside the benchmark

Contributors to a PAT benchmark are anonymous within it. A firm looking at benchmark context sees the shape of comparable results — not a list of named firms attached to numbers. A vendor seeing alignment context does not see which individual firms supplied which responses through the benchmark. The comparison picture is built from contributions, but it does not expose the contributors.

This anonymity is what lets benchmarks and honesty coexist. A firm mid-transformation, with an Automation result it is not yet proud of, can still contribute to the picture and still consult it — because contributing does not mean being identified, and consulting does not mean identifying anyone else. The backdrop is shared; the individual positions within it are private to their owners.

It is worth saying plainly what this means for a prospect weighing participation: joining PAT's benchmark picture is not an act of public disclosure. Your results inform a context that you and others can learn from, and your name is not pinned to them inside that context.

## When a benchmark refuses to show

Sometimes a benchmark cut simply does not appear — it shows as suppressed instead. This is PAT declining to publish a comparison that could not bear weight. A benchmark only publishes when its cohort has at least five contributors and no single contributor supplies more than a quarter of the data. Below that floor, or above that concentration, a "benchmark" would really be a thin sample — potentially guessable, potentially dominated by one participant's results, and in either case not the trustworthy backdrop it claims to be.

Suppression is therefore a form of honesty, and also a form of protection. A tiny comparison group is where anonymity gets weakest and where one contributor's numbers loom largest; refusing to publish those cuts protects both the reader from a misleading picture and the contributors from an exposing one. Seeing "suppressed" means the safeguard worked, not that something went wrong. The reasoning behind these thresholds — why small groups mislead and why dominance caps exist — is a subject in its own right, covered in PAT's article on benchmark integrity.

## A benchmark reading in practice

An illustrative walk-through makes the character of the thing concrete. Take a firm — invented for the example — that completes its assessment and scores 62 on Integration. On its own, 62 is a fact without surroundings. Where a benchmark cohort of comparable results has published, the firm can see its result against that backdrop: the same 62, now legible as a position within a landscape of real assessments rather than a number floating alone. Nothing in that view names another contributor, and nothing in it names this firm to anyone else. The firm learns where it sits; nobody learns where the firm sits. And like every PAT result, what leads the presentation is the band, with the 62 underneath in a supporting role — so the comparison reads as orientation, not as a scoreboard entry.

Now suppose the firm looks for a narrower comparison — a more specific cut that happens to draw on a small group. If that cohort has fewer than five contributors, or a single contributor supplies more than a quarter of its data, the cut shows as suppressed and the firm sees no backdrop there at all. The absence is the system working. A "context" assembled from a handful of results would be guessable and lopsided, and PAT declines to publish it — for this firm's protection as much as anyone's, since the firm's own future contributions deserve the same shelter.

What does appear can be trusted for a further reason: displayed numbers in PAT trace to their sources. A benchmark backdrop is not a decoration; it is an aggregate with a traceable ancestry, built only from production assessments that cleared the publication thresholds. Evidence lineage — no orphan numbers — applies to comparison context just as it applies to scores.

## Where richer benchmark context lives

PAT's membership comes in two paid tiers, Pro and Elite, and benchmark-flavored interpretation deepens with the tier. Pro covers the core of the experience: the assessment itself, scores, bands, and core insights. Elite adds interpretive surfaces — at the concept level, these include where a firm sits within a distribution of peers, how its scores are trending across assessments, and category-level context.

The distribution reading is the piece that touches benchmarks directly, and it obeys the same law as everything else on this page: it appears only when benchmarks publish under the suppression rules. Elite deepens interpretation; it does not buy a way around the safeguards. A cohort that is too small or too concentrated stays suppressed for every tier, and contributor anonymity holds at every tier. What a higher tier changes is how much interpretive help surrounds your own position — never how much of anyone else's position you can see.

That membership is paid-only, with no free tier, is part of the same design rather than a separate commercial fact: benchmark quality depends on committed participants, and both tiers represent that commitment. The tiers differ in depth of interpretation, not in depth of access to other people's data.

## What becomes of contributions over the long term

A firm weighing participation may reasonably ask a longer-horizon question: once my results have informed benchmark context, what is my ongoing exposure? PAT's data stewardship answers in two parts.

First, your data remains yours. Tenants' data belongs to them, and when a tenant leaves PAT, their data is removed — and the removal is receipted, so the exit is documented rather than taken on faith. Participation in comparison context is something a firm does, not something a firm becomes permanently subject to.

Second, the aggregates are genuinely aggregate. Anonymous benchmark aggregates that have already passed suppression remain aggregate: individual contributions are not reconstructable from them. The backdrop your results helped build carries no thread leading back to you. Contributing to a published benchmark is a one-way act of context-building, not a lingering identification risk.

It helps to remember, too, that the results feeding this picture are readings, not monuments. PAT treats a score as a reading at a point in time, and firms can re-take assessments as their operations change — so the comparison backdrop is built from how contributing firms actually stood when they answered, refreshed as participants assess again rather than fossilized from a single moment.

Together, these two commitments complete PAT's benchmark posture: contributing to the comparison picture never means becoming a target inside it — not while you participate, and not after you stop.

## No public shaming, by design

Put the pieces together and a consistent character emerges. No rankings means no public ordering to fall to the bottom of. Anonymity means no name attached to a disappointing number. Suppression means no thin cut through which an individual contributor could be singled out. And PAT's presentation style points the same direction: results lead with bands — named stages like Early or Leading — with raw numbers in a supporting role, which frames a result as a position on a journey rather than a mark to be judged by.

None of this is squeamishness about hard truths. PAT measures real gaps — including the alignment delta between what a vendor declares and what firms experience — and it reports what it measures in both directions. The commitment is narrower and more important: measurement is delivered to the people it belongs to, in context that helps them read it, and it is never turned into an instrument for embarrassing anyone in front of an audience. A platform asking firms to answer a hundred questions honestly owes them that, and benchmark quality depends on it. Shame-proof design is not a courtesy layered on top of the data — it is why the data is good.

## What feeds the picture — and what never does

Benchmark context is only as good as what is allowed into it, so PAT is strict about the inputs. Production, pilot, and demo data are kept strictly separate, and demo or synthetic data and pilot data never enter published benchmarks. The comparison backdrop you consult is built from real assessments by real participants, full stop.

Participation is part of the same logic. PAT has paid membership tiers only — Pro and Elite, with no free tier — because benchmark quality depends on committed participants. A comparison picture assembled from drive-by responses would not deserve anyone's trust; one assembled from members with a stake in accurate measurement can earn it. What you see in a PAT benchmark, in other words, is a picture that has been guarded at every gate: real data in, thin cuts refused, contributors anonymous, and no scoreboard anywhere.

## Why PAT works this way

Three of PAT's platform-wide design decisions meet in benchmarks. The first is deterministic scoring. A benchmark is an aggregate of measurements, and it can only be as trustworthy as the repeatability of what feeds it. PAT's scores come from deterministic arithmetic — the same answers always produce the same score — because a measurement must be repeatable and auditable; a score that could read the same answers differently on different days would be an opinion with a timestamp, not a measurement. A backdrop assembled from opinions would not deserve consulting; one assembled from repeatable measurements can.

The second is small-cell suppression, and here a sentence of orientation is enough: it is the same discipline statistical agencies practice, because below a minimum group size a "group" number is barely distinguishable from someone's private data, and a dominance cap keeps any single contributor's results from steering a number presented as a peer group. How the thresholds work in practice belongs to PAT's article on benchmark integrity; what matters here is that the discipline exists and is not negotiable.

The third is the absence of a free tier. Benchmark quality depends on committed participants; a free tier optimizes for volume over signal, and the numbers everyone compares against are only as good as the care behind the answers. Paid-only membership is, among other things, a benchmark-quality decision.

## How benchmark context looks for a firm, a vendor, and a consultant

For a firm, benchmark context is a backdrop behind its own results: a way to read a pillar score as a position in a landscape rather than a number floating alone. On Elite, that reading deepens into where the firm sits within a distribution of peers — only when the cohort publishes under the suppression rules.

For a vendor, context arrives through the alignment lens. Firm assessments measure experienced reality against declared capabilities, and the vendor receives structured, evidence-based views of where alignment holds and where it diverges — a comparison against its own declaration, across the firms experiencing the product, never a named list of which firm said what.

For a consultant, comparison is portfolio-shaped: reading alignment structure across a managed set. The rule this article keeps stating holds at that width too — context, never rankings.

## A closer look: one scenario, start to finish

Carry the earlier example — the invented firm that scored 62 on Integration — through a fuller arc. The firm completed its Integration module first, as the assessment's design allows, and saw a result led by its band, the 62 underneath in support. Against the published backdrop, the number gained surroundings: a position in a landscape, visible to the firm and to nobody else as the firm's.

The firm then looked for a narrower cut and found it suppressed — a comparison group too small or too concentrated to publish. Nothing was broken; the safeguard that will one day protect this firm's contributions was protecting someone else's.

Suppose the firm later moves to Elite. The interpretive surfaces deepen: where it sits within a distribution of peers, how its scores are trending across assessments, category-level context. None of that shows it anyone else's position; all of it obeys the same suppression rules.

Two quarters on, after real integration work, the firm re-assesses. Its new reading refreshes two things at once — its own position against the backdrop, and its contribution to the backdrop others consult, since the comparison picture is built from how contributing firms stood when they last answered. And if the firm ever left PAT entirely, its data would be removed with a receipted removal, while aggregates that had already passed suppression would stay aggregate, with no thread leading back. At every stage of this arc the firm learned about itself; at no stage did anyone learn about the firm.

## Common misconceptions

"A benchmark tells you your rank." It does not, because there is no rank to tell — PAT maintains no ordered list of firms or vendors anywhere. Benchmark context shows where a result sits within a landscape of comparable results: a position you can learn, not a placing anyone can publish.

"Consulting a benchmark shows you who else is in it." Contributors are anonymous within a benchmark. You see the shape of comparable results, never names attached to numbers — and nobody consulting the same backdrop sees yours.

"Contributing to a benchmark is a form of public disclosure." It is not. Your results inform an anonymous aggregate that must clear publication thresholds before appearing, and published aggregates are not reconstructable back to contributors.

"Elite membership unlocks more of other participants' data." Elite deepens interpretation of your own position — distribution placement, trajectory, category context. It buys no path around suppression and no view of anyone else.

"A benchmark is a target your firm is supposed to hit." A benchmark is context for interpretation, not a verdict or a quota. It helps you read your own result; what to do about that reading remains your call — now made with surroundings.
