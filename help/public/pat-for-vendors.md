For a software vendor serving accounting firms, PAT is a source of something marketing cannot manufacture: independent evidence of how your product's declared capabilities meet the reality firms actually experience. You declare, in structured form, what your product is built to do; firm assessments measure how those areas of practice actually perform; and PAT returns the alignment picture — including the welcome cases where your product is doing more than you claimed for it.

## What a vendor declares

Participation begins with a structured declaration. Rather than free-form marketing copy, you state your product's capabilities in PAT's structured format — a disciplined account of what the product does for the firms that run it. The structure matters because it makes your claims measurable: a declaration expressed in comparable terms is one that firm-side evidence can actually be held against, area by area.

This is the vendor's half of PAT's founding idea, "evidence, not advertising." A declaration is not a promotional surface; it is the fixed reference point that everything you learn afterward is measured from. The more honestly it is drawn, the more useful every subsequent reading becomes.

## Declarations organized by function area

A declaration is not one monolithic claim. Vendors declare capabilities across defined function areas of practice software, so what you produce is a structured map: for each defined area your product serves, a stated capability. Firm product assessments then measure experienced reality against that map area by area, and the alignment views you receive back keep the same granularity. That granularity is what makes the evidence actionable. Alignment can hold in one function area and diverge in another — a product can be experienced exactly as declared in one part of its footprint while running ahead of or behind its declaration elsewhere — and area-level evidence keeps those findings distinct instead of blending them into a single satisfaction figure that would tell you nothing about where to look.

## The alignment evidence you receive

On the other side of your declaration sit the firms. Accounting firms assess their own operations through PAT's structured assessments, answering consistent questions about how the areas of their practice actually perform — including the areas your product serves. The measured gap between your declared capability and firms' assessed experience of it is the alignment delta, and it is the core of what a vendor receives.

The delta flows both ways, and this deserves emphasis. A product can underperform its declaration, and knowing that early — from measurement rather than from churn — is worth a great deal. But a product can also outperform its declaration: firms may be experiencing more value in an area than you claimed for it, which tells you your own account of the product is selling it short. Either direction, the evidence is specific enough to act on, because it is anchored to declared capabilities rather than to general sentiment.

What makes this evidence credible is the discipline underneath it. Firm-side scores come from deterministic arithmetic on a consistent question set — no AI model sits in the scoring path, so the same answers always produce the same score. Results carry signal-integrity checks, so a reading built on weak response patterns is flagged as such rather than silently counted; an integrity flag is information about how much weight a result can bear, never an accusation aimed at anyone. And every number you see traces to its sources — evidence lineage is a platform rule, and PAT publishes no orphan numbers.

## How the evidence reads

The firm assessments that answer your declaration are structured instruments, and the evidence you receive inherits their vocabulary. Firms score on a 0–100 scale across five pillars of practice operations — Operations, Automation, Integration, Governance, and Strategy — and results present through five bands: Early, Developing, Building, Established, Leading. Bands lead the presentation and raw numbers support it, which serves a vendor well: a banded reading communicates where firm experience stands in terms your product, sales, and leadership teams can all use in the same meeting, while the underlying numbers remain available when precision matters.

Because the firm-side instrument is deliberately consistent — the same questions, the same scale, the same arithmetic for every firm — the experience readings that meet your declaration are comparable in a way ad hoc feedback never is. Two firms describing the same area of practice were asked the same things in the same words. When their assessed experience converges, that convergence means something; when it scatters, the scatter is itself a finding.

## One declaration, two directions: an illustration

Consider an invented vendor, purely for illustration, with a practice-software product declared across several defined function areas. When alignment evidence arrives, three patterns are possible, and a real vendor will often see all three at once.

In the first function area, firms' assessed experience sits close to the declaration. That is verification — independent, measured confirmation that this part of the product does what the vendor says it does, which no amount of the vendor's own material could establish.

In the second area, experienced reality reads meaningfully below what was declared. The alignment delta points downward, and importantly, the measurement is not a verdict on why. The gap might live in the product, in how firms adopted or configured it, or in a declaration drawn more optimistically than the evidence supports. The delta says where to look; the product team decides what it finds there. Learning this from measurement, while it is still just information, is a very different experience from learning it later from attrition.

In the third area, firms' assessed experience reads above the declaration: they are getting more from that part of the product than the vendor claimed for it. The vendor's own account has been underselling something real — a finding as commercially useful as any gap, and one that only a two-directional measurement could surface. The names and areas here are invented; the both-ways mechanism is exactly how the platform is designed.

## Comparison context with real protections

Alignment evidence also comes with context. PAT benchmarks let you see readings against comparison cohorts — but benchmarks are context, never rankings, and the protections that shield firms shield vendors equally. There are no league tables. Contributors are anonymous within benchmarks. A benchmark cut publishes only when at least five contributors stand behind it and no single contributor supplies more than a quarter of its data; anything short of that shows as suppressed rather than publishing a number too thin to trust.

The data behind those comparisons is kept clean by hard boundaries: production, pilot, and demo data are strictly separated, and demo, synthetic, and pilot data never enter published benchmarks. Evidence about how your product lands in the real world is drawn only from the real world.

## Evidence for honest conversations

What a vendor receives is structured and evidence-based by design, and that shape is chosen for a reason: it is meant to be usable in honest sales and product conversations. In a sales conversation, alignment evidence changes the footing. Where alignment holds, you can say so with measurement behind the statement rather than assertion — the declared capability and the firm-experienced reality, side by side. Where the evidence diverges, you know it before your prospect discovers it, which is the difference between an honest conversation and an awkward one. In product conversations, the same evidence gives prioritization discussions a factual floor: the areas where experience runs behind declaration are identified by measurement, not by whichever anecdote arrived loudest. This is the vendor-side payoff of "evidence, not advertising" — not that advertising disappears, but that what you say about your product can rest on something verified.

## The discipline behind the instrument that measures you

A vendor being measured has a legitimate interest in how the measuring instrument is governed, and PAT's answer is deliberately strict. Assessment and learning module content must cite authoritative sources to enter the system at all — unsourced content is rejected mechanically at import — and content goes live only after a named human reviewer signs off, with the review recorded: who reviewed it, and when. The questions through which firms assess their experience of your product are therefore sourced, reviewed, and accountable, and the scoring that follows them is deterministic arithmetic applied identically to every firm. When alignment evidence arrives on your desk, the instrument that produced it can explain itself all the way down.

## Your data, and what leaving would mean

PAT's data stewardship applies to vendors as it does to every tenant: your data belongs to you. Your declarations and vendor-side data are yours, and if you ever leave the platform, that data is removed — with the removal receipted, not merely promised. What persists afterward is only what was already anonymous and aggregate: benchmark figures that passed PAT's suppression rules remain aggregate, and no individual contribution can be reconstructed from them. The same boundary that protects firms' candor protects your participation — nothing that outlives your membership can be traced back to you.

## What you see, and what you don't

PAT is built on role boundaries, and vendors sit inside them like everyone else. As a vendor you see your own declarations and the alignment evidence that answers them; you do not see into individual firms' private assessment detail, and firms within benchmarks remain anonymous to you. Firms, for their part, see what their own role permits — not your vendor-side surfaces. Those walls are what make honest participation safe on both sides: firms can answer candidly because candor is not exposure, and the candor is exactly what gives the evidence you receive its weight.

Signed-in vendor users also have Ask Pat, an assistant that answers questions from PAT's own documented content, scoped at the data layer to what a vendor role can see. Pat is there to help you understand your evidence — what a term means, how to read a surface — and when it lacks documented evidence for an answer it says so plainly. It never generates scores; nothing conversational ever touches the arithmetic.

## Why vendors participate

The case for a vendor is straightforward. Every product team already carries beliefs about how its product performs in the field, assembled from support tickets, sales anecdotes, and the loudest voices in the room. PAT replaces that assemblage with measurement: a structured declaration on one side, verified firm experience on the other, and the distance between them stated plainly. Where the evidence confirms your declaration, you hold something advertising cannot buy — independent verification. Where it diverges, in either direction, you have found something worth understanding while it is still just information.

There is also a longer arc. A declaration measured once is a snapshot; a declaration measured against firm experience again and again becomes a record of how your product's reality tracks its stated capability as both evolve. Consultants and ecosystem owners, who manage sets of firms and the products in play across them, look at this same alignment structure from their own seat — which means the evidence answering your declaration is part of the picture the ecosystem's most influential advisors are working from. Being measured well, in that company, is its own form of standing.

Vendor participation, like all PAT membership, is paid — the Pro and Elite tiers, with Elite adding deeper interpretive surfaces such as position within a distribution and trajectory over time. There is no free tier, by design: evidence of this kind depends on committed participants on both sides of the measurement, and the vendors and firms who fund the platform are the reason its benchmarks are worth comparing against.

## Why PAT works this way

For a vendor weighing participation, three of PAT's design choices carry most of the argument.

Deterministic scoring is what makes the evidence about your product auditable. Model behavior can shift over time, and a score that could read the same answers differently on different days is an opinion with a timestamp, not a measurement. Because firm-side scores are pure arithmetic on a consistent question set, the readings that meet your declaration are repeatable: the same answers produce the same result, this quarter or next, and anyone examining the evidence can retrace how it was computed.

The sourced-content gate is what makes the instrument itself accountable. In a profession built on standards, content is only as trustworthy as its sources — so assessment content that cannot cite authoritative sources is rejected mechanically at import, and nothing serves until a named human reviewer has signed off, with the sign-off recorded. The questions through which firms assess your product's footprint earned their place before they were allowed to measure anything.

The absence of a free tier is what keeps the evidence worth having. A free tier optimizes for volume over signal, and the numbers everyone compares against are only as good as the care behind the answers. Because every participant on both sides of the measurement is a committed member, the firm experience readings that answer your declaration come from practices with something at stake — which is precisely what gives them their weight.

## A closer look: a declaration through a full cycle

Stay with the invented vendor from the earlier illustration and follow one full cycle. The declaration is drawn honestly, area by area, across the defined function areas the product serves, and becomes the fixed reference point. Over the following months, firms running the product complete their assessments at their own pace, and the alignment evidence assembles: verification in the first function area, experience reading below declaration in the second, experience reading above it in the third.

The vendor's product team takes the second area's gap as a question rather than a verdict — is it the product, the adoption, or a declaration drawn too optimistically? — and works the area. The commercial team, meanwhile, begins speaking differently about the third area, because measured firm experience shows the product delivering more there than its own account had claimed. When the next reading cycle comes around, firms re-assess as their operations change, and the fresh delta shows whether the second area's gap narrowed. Throughout, every reading traveled with its signal-integrity information, any benchmark context either passed the contributor floor and dominance cap or showed as suppressed, and every figure could trace its lineage to its sources. And where the product sits inside consultant-managed ecosystems, the alignment picture it earns is part of what those advisors see from their own seat. The vendor and its function areas are invented; the cycle is the platform working as designed.

## Common misconceptions

"A more generous declaration produces better-looking evidence." The opposite. A declaration is the fixed reference point everything is measured from, so an overstated declaration simply manufactures gaps between claim and experience. The more honestly a declaration is drawn, the more useful every reading that answers it becomes.

"The alignment delta only measures shortfall." The delta flows both ways by design. Firms' assessed experience can read above a declaration just as easily as below it, and the outperform finding — a product delivering more than its own account claims — is surfaced with the same rigor as any gap.

"Vendors can see which firms produced the readings." They cannot. Firms are anonymous within benchmarks, and individual firms' private assessment detail sits behind role walls enforced at the data layer. That protection is not a limitation on the evidence; it is what makes firms candid enough for the evidence to mean anything.

"Alignment evidence is a rating PAT assigns to your product." PAT assigns nothing. The evidence is the measured distance between your own structured declaration and firms' assessed experience — both sides supplied by participants, with deterministic arithmetic doing the rest. There is no editorial judgment, panel opinion, or model interpretation anywhere in it.
