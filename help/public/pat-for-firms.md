For an accounting firm, PAT is a way to see your own operations clearly: a structured five-pillar assessment that produces comparable scores across the areas that matter, presented in plain bands, with honest comparison context alongside. What you get is not advice or a sales pitch — it is a measured picture of where your practice stands, one you can revisit as often as your firm changes.

## An operational alignment view of your own firm

Most firms know their operations the way you know a house you live in — intimately, but unevenly. Some rooms get attention every day; others nobody has opened in years. PAT gives a firm a single structured view across the whole practice, built from your own answers to consistent questions, so the areas you rarely examine get measured with the same care as the ones you think about constantly.

The result is an alignment view in two senses. Internally, you see how the different areas of your operations compare with one another on a common scale — where your practice is strongest, and where the readings sit lower. Externally, your assessed experience of the products you use becomes part of a larger evidence picture: PAT measures the gap between what vendors declare their products do and what firms like yours actually experience, and your firm's honest answers are what make that measurement real. You contribute experience; the ecosystem gets evidence.

## Five pillars, one measuring stick

A firm assessment covers five pillars of practice operations: Operations, which looks at your operating model and workflow discipline; Automation, covering automation and AI readiness; Integration, covering integration and data flow maturity; Governance, spanning governance, controls, and vendor risk; and Strategy, covering strategy, change readiness, and market alignment.

Each pillar is assessed through its own module of twenty questions, one hundred questions in all, and the questions are answered on a consistent numeric scale. The assessment is deliberately built so the five resulting scores can be compared side by side — the same measuring stick applied to five different rooms of the house. That comparability is the point: a firm rarely needs to be told it has weaknesses in the abstract; what it needs is to see which areas sit lower than the others, by how much, and whether that picture shifts over time.

## An assessment that fits the rhythm of a working firm

A five-module, 100-question structured assessment sounds like a commitment, and PAT is deliberate about not letting it become an ordeal. The flow is paged, with autosave: a partner can answer for twenty minutes, stop, and resume later exactly where they left off. Progress is tracked per module, and the five modules can be completed in any order over whatever stretch of time suits the firm — there is no requirement to finish in a sitting, or even in a month. Results update as modules complete, so a firm that has finished three modules already has three readable pillar scores while the remaining two wait their turn. Within each module, questions are organized into sections, which keeps a twenty-question module from feeling like an undifferentiated wall.

The pacing philosophy extends beyond the assessment itself. PAT paces engagement deliberately — quarterly rhythms are the kind of cadence it encourages — and its reminders are consciously no-guilt: informative nudges about where things stand, never shame mechanics. The platform wants a firm to come back because the next reading is useful, not because a red badge made someone feel behind.

## Room for your own words

Alongside the twenty scored questions, each module carries a small set of open-ended questions. These carry zero score weight — they are qualitative only — and that is a deliberate boundary: the arithmetic stays clean, driven entirely by the consistent numeric scale, while the open text gives context in the firm's own words. The pairing matters more than it first appears. A score can tell you that an area reads lower than the others; your own written observations, captured at the moment of assessment, preserve what was actually going on — which workflow was straining, which handoff kept breaking — in language no numeric scale could hold. When the firm returns to its results later, the numbers say what the reading was, and the words say what it felt like from inside.

## Scores you can actually read

Every pillar score is computed by deterministic arithmetic on a 0–100 scale. No AI model sits anywhere in the scoring path, which means the same answers always produce the same score — your result is a calculation, not an interpretation, and it cannot drift between one day and the next.

What you see first, though, is not a number but a band. PAT presents results through five bands — Early, Developing, Building, Established, Leading — with the raw number in a supporting role. A band tells you where a pillar stands in terms a partner meeting can use without a translator; the number underneath is there when you want the finer grain.

Your scores also travel with signal integrity information: quality checks on response patterns accompany results so you know how much weight a reading can bear. If a module was answered in a way that weakens the signal — rushed, say, with the same response given down the page — the score says so honestly rather than pretending to a confidence it has not earned. An integrity flag is information about the data, never an accusation about the people who supplied it.

## Five readings, side by side: an illustration

Consider an invented firm, purely for illustration. Its completed assessment comes back reading Established on Operations, Building on Automation, Building on Integration with a supporting score of 62, Developing on Governance, and Established on Strategy. What the leadership team sees first is the shape: two areas Established, two Building, and Governance sitting a band below the rest. That shape is the conversation — nobody needs a methodology briefing to ask why Governance reads lower than everything else.

The supporting numbers earn their keep at the next level of detail. Automation and Integration share a band, but the raw scores beneath them show which sits higher within it and by how much — useful when deciding where attention goes first. If the Governance module happened to be answered in a rush, with the same response running straight down the page, a signal-integrity flag would accompany that score, telling the team to treat the Developing reading as a weaker signal and perhaps worth a more careful retake. And if the firm looks for a benchmark cut that has not yet met PAT's publication standards — at least five contributors, none supplying more than a quarter of the data — that cut shows as suppressed rather than offering a comparison too thin to mean anything. Every figure in this example is invented; the reading experience is exactly this concrete.

## Comparison context without exposure

Once your firm has scores, the natural question is: compared to what? PAT answers with benchmarks — comparison context, never rankings. There is no league table, no published ordering of firms, no way for your firm to be named or shamed by its results. Contributors to a benchmark are anonymous within it, and a benchmark is only published at all when enough independent contributors stand behind it; a cut that cannot meet PAT's publication standards shows as suppressed instead.

The comparison data itself is kept clean by strict boundaries: production, pilot, and demo data are separated, and synthetic or pilot data never enters published benchmarks. When your firm's Established reading on Integration sits alongside a benchmark, both sides of that comparison are real. Suppression may occasionally mean a cut you wanted to see is not available yet — and that is the design working, because a comparison too thin to trust would be worse for your firm than no comparison at all.

## A reading at a point in time, not a label

PAT treats a score as a reading at a point in time, never a permanent label. Assessments can be re-taken as operations change — after a new workflow beds in, after an integration project lands, after a leadership change — and each retake applies the same measuring stick to the firm as it now is. The long-term reading PAT is designed for is band movement: a firm re-assessing across quarters can see whether an area actually moved — Governance going from Developing to Building, say — rather than relying on the general feeling that things have improved. Movement in either direction is information. An area that slipped a band is worth understanding just as much as one that climbed, and because the questions, scale, and arithmetic never change between readings, the movement you see is movement in the firm, not noise in the instrument.

## Your data belongs to your firm

The answers a firm gives PAT are the firm's own, and the platform's data stewardship reflects that. Tenant data belongs to the tenant: if your firm ever leaves, its data is removed, and the removal is receipted rather than merely promised. What persists is only what was already beyond reach — anonymous benchmark aggregates that passed PAT's suppression rules stay aggregate, and no individual firm's contribution can be reconstructed from them. In practice this means a firm can answer the assessment with full candor, contribute honestly to the comparison context every member depends on, and know that nothing which outlives its participation can be traced back to it.

## Insight that deepens as you engage

PAT membership comes in two paid tiers, Pro and Elite — there is no free tier, by design, because the quality of everyone's comparison context depends on committed participants. Elite adds deeper interpretive surfaces on top of Pro: where your firm sits within a distribution rather than just against a midpoint, and how your trajectory has moved over time rather than just where you stand today.

The assessment itself can also deepen. Beyond the five core modules, additional short modules can open based on your firm's scoring pattern — a diagnostic module to probe a weak area, a strength module to understand why something is working, or a remediation module for guided improvement. All such content is reviewed and sourced before it can serve, so anything that opens for your firm carries the same discipline as the core assessment.

And whenever the results raise questions, Ask Pat is available: an assistant that answers from PAT's own documented content, scoped to what your role can see. Pat explains; it never scores. When it lacks documented evidence for a question, it tells you so rather than guessing — which is precisely the behavior you would want from anything standing next to your firm's numbers.

## What a firm does with all this

The value of the assessment is less any single score than the conversations it makes possible. Five comparable readings give a leadership team a shared, neutral vocabulary: instead of arguing from impressions, you can point at the same picture and discuss why Governance reads a band below Operations, or what changed since the last assessment. Reassessing over time turns a snapshot into a trendline. And because your assessed experience feeds the broader alignment evidence, the products you rely on are measured against what they declared — with your firm's reality as part of the proof, in either direction.

It is worth being clear about what PAT does not do for a firm. It does not hand down a verdict, prescribe a transformation program, or promise that a score movement translates into any particular business result. PAT measures, and what a firm does with the measurement is the firm's own work. What PAT commits to is that the measurement will be consistent, comparable, honestly presented, and traceable to its sources — and that when you come back in a year, the same measuring stick will be waiting.

## Why PAT works this way

Three design decisions shape the firm experience described above, and each has a reason worth knowing.

The repeated questions are the instrument. A measurement instrument must stay constant so that only the lens changes: because every module asks the same twenty questions in identical wording, a difference between your Integration and Governance scores can only come from your operations, not from how a question happened to be phrased. If wording varied per pillar, score differences could be wording artifacts — an instrument problem masquerading as a finding. PAT changes the lens, never the instrument.

Bands lead because single-point scores invite false precision. A 0–100 number carries an air of exactness that no structured self-assessment can honestly support down to the last digit, so PAT leads with the band — the statement the measurement can genuinely stand behind — and keeps the raw number in a supporting role. The reading that matters most over the long run is movement between bands, which is why the whole presentation is built around them.

Suppression is borrowed from statistical practice. The rule that a benchmark cut needs at least five contributors, with no single contributor supplying more than a quarter of the data, is the same small-cell discipline statistical agencies use: below a minimum group size, a "group" number is barely distinguishable from someone's private data, and the dominance cap stops any one contributor's results from steering a figure presented as a peer group. When a cut you wanted shows as suppressed, that discipline is protecting your firm too.

## A closer look: the same firm, two quarters on

Return to the invented firm from the earlier illustration — Established on Operations and Strategy, Building on Automation and Integration, Developing on Governance — and follow it forward. The leadership team decides to spend a quarter on the area that read lowest. Because the Governance score arrived with a signal-integrity note about rushed answering, the firm's first step is the careful retake that flag suggested, giving that module the unhurried attention the first pass lacked. Suppose the careful reading still comes back Developing: now the team trusts the reading and can act on it, rather than wondering whether it was an artifact of a hurried afternoon.

The firm's scoring pattern may also open an adaptive module — a short diagnostic module probing the weak area, or later a remediation module for guided improvement — each built from sourced, human-reviewed content like everything else in the system. The open-ended answers the partners wrote at first assessment, describing which handoff kept breaking and which control existed on paper only, sit alongside the numbers as context in the firm's own words. Two quarters later the firm re-assesses Governance and reads Building. On an Elite membership, the trajectory view now shows that movement across readings, and if a relevant benchmark cohort has met the publication thresholds, the firm can see where its new reading sits within a distribution of anonymous peers. Every name and number here is invented; the sequence is the intended use of the platform.

## Common misconceptions

"The assessment has to be finished in one sitting." It does not. The flow is paged with autosave, progress is tracked per module, and the five modules can be completed in any order over whatever stretch of time suits the firm — with results updating as each module completes.

"An integrity flag means our people are accused of dishonesty." An integrity flag is information about how much weight a reading can bear, never an accusation. It tells you the response pattern weakened the signal — and a careful retake resolves the question.

"Other members can see our results." No other firm, vendor, or consultant sees your firm's results beyond what role boundaries permit, contributors to benchmarks are anonymous within them, and no published ordering of firms exists anywhere in the product.

"A Developing band is a verdict on the firm." A band is a reading at a point in time, not a label. The intended long-term reading is movement — whether an area shifts band between assessments — and movement in either direction is information, not judgment.

"Pro members get less reliable scores than Elite members." The measurement is identical for every member: the same questions, scale, and deterministic arithmetic. Elite differs in interpretive surfaces around the results — distribution position, trajectory over time — never in the scoring itself.
