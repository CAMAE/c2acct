## SECTION A — Matter Intake, Engagement, Conflicts and Scope (25: 8E/12M/5H)

**A1 (E)** A three-attorney firm's intake script asks a caller only for the names of the parties and the general nature of the matter before a conflict check is run. That practice tracks:
a) Rule 1.18 Comment 4, which suggests limiting the initial consultation to what is needed to check for conflicts b) Rule 1.5, which requires the fee basis be stated at first contact c) Rule 1.3, which requires prompt action on every inquiry d) Rule 1.16, which governs declining a representation — **a.** Taking less information at first contact lowers the chance of receiving disqualifying information from a person who never becomes a client. *[ABA Model Rule 1.18, Comment 4]*

**A2 (E)** Rule 1.7 Comment 3 says a firm's procedures for identifying conflicts of interest should be:
a) the same at every firm regardless of size b) appropriate for the size and type of firm and the nature of its practice c) required only at firms that litigate d) optional at firms with fewer than ten lawyers — **b.** The comment scales the procedure to the firm and practice rather than prescribing one design. *[ABA Model Rule 1.7, Comment 3]*

**A3 (M)** A 40-lawyer firm discovers a conflict after the engagement letter is signed. Intake records show that no conflict check was run because the originating partner "knew the client." Under Rule 1.7 Comment 3, the missing check:
a) is irrelevant if no harm to either client resulted b) is excused because the partner had personal knowledge c) does not excuse the violation, because ignorance caused by a failure to institute reasonable procedures is not a defense d) shifts responsibility to the client who did not disclose the adverse party — **c.** The comment states that ignorance caused by a failure to institute conflict procedures will not excuse a violation. *[ABA Model Rule 1.7, Comment 3]*

**A4 (M)** A client gives oral consent to a concurrent conflict during a phone call while the lawyer is traveling and cannot send a confirming writing that day. Rule 1.7 Comment 20 treats the writing as:
a) unnecessary when the client is a sophisticated business b) satisfied by a note in the lawyer's own file c) deferred until the matter closes d) required within a reasonable time after the oral consent when it is not feasible to obtain or transmit it at the time — **d.** "Confirmed in writing" allows a lawyer-transmitted writing promptly after oral consent, with a reasonable-time backstop. *[ABA Model Rule 1.7(b)(4) and Comment 20; Rule 1.0 definition of "confirmed in writing"]*

**A5 (H)** A large regional firm's standard engagement letter contains a general, open-ended advance consent to all future conflicts with any affiliate of the client. Under Rule 1.7 Comment 22 that consent is:
a) ordinarily ineffective because the client is unlikely to understand the material risks involved b) effective for any client that signs it c) effective only when the future matter is litigation d) required by Rule 1.7 for institutional clients — **a.** Advance consent is more likely effective when it is specific to a type of conflict the client understands and the client is experienced or independently advised. *[ABA Model Rule 1.7, Comment 22]*

**A6 (M)** A small firm (6 to 20) is asked to represent a buyer against a former client in a dispute over the same acquisition the firm handled for that former client two years ago. Rule 1.9(a) conditions the new representation on:
a) nothing, because the former relationship has ended b) the former client's informed consent, confirmed in writing, because the matters are the same or substantially related c) a fee credit to the former client d) written notice to the tribunal — **b.** A lawyer may not take a substantially related matter adverse to a former client without that client's informed consent confirmed in writing. *[ABA Model Rule 1.9(a)]*

**A7 (E)** Rule 1.9 Comment 3 describes matters as "substantially related" when:
a) they fall in the same practice area b) the same lawyer worked on both matters c) they involve the same transaction or legal dispute, or there is a substantial risk that confidential factual information from the prior matter would materially advance the new client's position d) both clients operate in the same industry — **c.** The test looks at the transaction or dispute and at the risk that protected information would be used, not at practice area. *[ABA Model Rule 1.9, Comment 3]*

**A8 (H)** A midsize firm hires a lateral associate whose former firm represents the opposing party in one of the midsize firm's active matters. Under ABA Model Rule 1.10(a)(2), the firm's lateral-onboarding procedure can avoid imputation by:
a) obtaining an oral assurance from the lateral that no information will be shared b) waiting 90 days before staffing the lateral on any matter c) obtaining consent from the firm's own client only d) timely screening the lateral with no share of the fee, giving prompt written notice to the affected former client describing the screen, and providing certifications of compliance on request and at termination of the screen — **d.** The Model Rule permits nonconsensual screening of a lateral only with fee segregation, notice and certifications; several states use a different formulation. *[ABA Model Rule 1.10(a)(2); state variants differ, e.g. Minnesota Rule 1.10(b); state-specific]*

**A9 (M)** A firm's web intake form logs every inbound message as a prospective client for conflicts purposes. Rule 1.18 Comment 2 says a person who:
a) sends unsolicited information in response to general advertising, without a reasonable expectation that the lawyer is willing to discuss a client-lawyer relationship, is not a prospective client b) speaks with a receptionist is always a prospective client c) pays a consultation fee is not a prospective client d) is later declined loses the protections of the rule — **a.** The rule protects people who consult about forming a relationship, not everyone who communicates unilaterally. *[ABA Model Rule 1.18(a) and Comment 2]*

**A10 (H)** A lawyer at a solo and small firm received information that could be significantly harmful from a prospective client who then hired other counsel. The firm now wants to represent the adverse party in the same matter. Rule 1.18(c) and (d) treat that as:
a) permitted without further steps because no engagement was formed b) barred unless both the prospective client and the affected client give informed consent confirmed in writing, or the lawyer took reasonable measures to avoid exposure to more disqualifying information than necessary and is timely screened with written notice to the prospective client c) barred permanently with no exception d) permitted with a fee refund to the prospective client — **b.** Rule 1.18 imputes the disqualification firm-wide unless the consent or the screening path is satisfied. *[ABA Model Rule 1.18(c) and (d)]*

**A11 (E)** A plaintiff contingency firm offers document-review-only engagements. Rule 1.2(c) permits limiting the scope of a representation when:
a) the client pays the fee in advance b) the matter is not in litigation c) the limitation is reasonable under the circumstances and the client gives informed consent d) a supervising partner signs the intake form — **c.** Limited-scope representation is allowed on those two conditions. *[ABA Model Rule 1.2(c)]*

**A12 (M)** A consumer practice sells a 15-minute phone consultation as a limited-scope service. Rule 1.2 Comment 7 cautions that:
a) the duty of competence does not apply to limited engagements b) limited scope may exclude confidentiality c) limited-scope services are available only to repeat clients d) the limitation must still be reasonable, and a brief consultation is appropriate only if there is enough time for reliable advice — **d.** Limited representation still requires competent work within its boundaries. *[ABA Model Rule 1.2, Comment 7]*

**A13 (E)** Rule 1.5(b) says the scope of the representation and the basis or rate of the fee and expenses are communicated to the client:
a) preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after commencing the representation b) only if the client asks c) on the first invoice d) at the close of the matter — **a.** The rule sets the timing and a preference for writing; it does not require a signed document for every hourly engagement. *[ABA Model Rule 1.5(b)]*

**A14 (M)** A litigation firm confirms a contingent fee arrangement by an email exchange and never obtains a signed document. Rule 1.5(c) requires a contingent fee agreement to be:
a) oral if both sides agree b) in a writing signed by the client that states the method of calculation, the percentages at settlement, trial and appeal, and how expenses are treated c) filed with the court d) approved by a second partner — **b.** The rule names the writing, the signature and the contents. *[ABA Model Rule 1.5(c)]*

**A15 (E)** At the conclusion of a contingent fee matter, Rule 1.5(c) requires the client to receive:
a) a copy of every pleading filed b) an oral summary of the result c) a written statement of the outcome and, if there is a recovery, the remittance to the client and the method of its determination d) an itemized time record for the matter — **c.** The closing statement is part of the contingent fee rule. *[ABA Model Rule 1.5(c)]*

**A16 (M)** A 600-lawyer firm's intake system will not issue a matter number until an engagement letter stating scope and fee basis is attached. That gate most directly operationalizes:
a) Rule 3.3 on candor to the tribunal b) FRCP 26(f) conference requirements c) Rule 1.15(b) on lawyer funds in trust d) Rule 1.5(b) on communicating the fee basis and Rule 1.2(c) on scope — **d.** A matter-open gate is a systems-level way to meet the timing and content expectations of those rules. *[ABA Model Rule 1.5(b); Rule 1.2(c)]*

**A17 (M)** A midsize firm is discharged by a client in the middle of a litigated matter. Under Rule 1.16(a) and (c), the firm:
a) must withdraw, subject to any requirement to obtain the tribunal's permission b) may continue until outstanding fees are paid c) may hold the file until the invoice is settled d) has 30 days to decide whether to continue — **a.** Discharge is a mandatory withdrawal ground; a tribunal's rules on notice and permission still apply. *[ABA Model Rule 1.16(a)(3) and (c)]*

**A18 (E)** Which step appears in Rule 1.16(d)'s list of duties on termination of a representation?
a) charging a file-closing fee b) refunding any advance payment of fee or expense that has not been earned or incurred c) keeping all originals for one year d) notifying the state bar of the termination — **b.** The rule also requires reasonable notice, time to obtain other counsel, and surrender of papers and property. *[ABA Model Rule 1.16(d)]*

**A19 (M)** A small firm wants to withdraw from a matter because the client has not paid for four months despite repeated written warnings. Rule 1.16(b) treats that situation as:
a) a mandatory withdrawal ground b) never a ground for withdrawal c) a permissive ground, when the client fails substantially to fulfill an obligation after reasonable warning that the lawyer will withdraw d) a ground only when the unpaid balance exceeds a set amount — **c.** Nonpayment after warning is one of the listed permissive grounds. *[ABA Model Rule 1.16(b)(5)]*

**A20 (H)** Rule 5.1 Comment 2 names internal policies and procedures that partners should have in place. Which set matches the comment?
a) marketing approval, billing rates and office hours b) social media, dress code and parking c) cloud vendor selection, encryption and backups d) detecting and resolving conflicts of interest, identifying dates by which actions must be taken, accounting for client funds and property, and supervising inexperienced lawyers — **d.** The comment ties the supervisory duty to those four operational systems. *[ABA Model Rule 5.1, Comment 2]*

**A21 (M)** Rule 5.1 Comment 3 says a small firm of experienced lawyers may satisfy the duty to have firm measures through:
a) informal supervision and periodic review b) no supervision at all c) an outside compliance officer d) an annual external audit — **a.** Larger firms or firms handling intensive ethical problems may need more elaborate measures. *[ABA Model Rule 5.1, Comment 3]*

**A22 (E)** A client emails asking for the status of a matter. Under Rule 1.4(a)(4) and Comment 4, the lawyer should:
a) reply at the next billing cycle b) promptly comply with the reasonable request, or acknowledge it and say when a response will come c) direct the client to the portal and take no further step d) wait until there is a development to report — **b.** The rule requires prompt compliance or a prompt acknowledgment. *[ABA Model Rule 1.4(a)(4) and Comment 4]*

**A23 (M)** A 12-lawyer firm signs engagement letters through an e-signature workflow. Under the ESIGN Act, an electronically signed engagement letter:
a) is invalid unless notarized b) requires a paper duplicate to be enforceable c) may not be denied legal effect, validity or enforceability solely because it is in electronic form d) must be filed with a court to take effect — **c.** ESIGN gives electronic signatures and records the same legal effect as paper for transactions in interstate commerce. *[15 U.S.C. 7001(a); ESIGN Act]*

**A24 (H)** A consumer-facing firm delivers legally required written disclosures to individual clients electronically. ESIGN section 7001(c) conditions that delivery on:
a) a paper waiver signed at the first meeting b) a court order permitting electronic delivery c) an email address on file d) the consumer's affirmative consent, given in a way that reasonably demonstrates ability to access the electronic form, after a clear and conspicuous statement of the right to paper and the right to withdraw — **d.** Consumer consent under ESIGN is affirmative, informed and demonstrable, not implied. *[15 U.S.C. 7001(c); ESIGN Act]*

**A25 (M)** The Green Book's Principle 3 (establish structure, responsibility and authority), read for a law firm's intake process, corresponds to:
a) naming who may open a matter, who approves engagement terms, and who clears conflicts b) selecting the firm's bank c) setting hourly rates by seniority d) choosing the venue for a dispute — **a.** Assigning responsibility and authority for each control point is the transferable principle. *[GAO Green Book 2025, Principle 3, adapted to firm context]*

## SECTION B — Calendaring, Docketing, Deadlines and Work Assignment (22: 7E/11M/4H)

**B1 (E)** Rule 1.3 Comment 3 identifies the deadline failure that can destroy a client's legal position. It is:
a) a late monthly invoice b) missing a statute of limitations c) a delayed status letter d) a lapsed continuing education credit — **b.** The comment says procrastination can prejudice a client, and a missed limitations period can destroy the position. *[ABA Model Rule 1.3, Comment 3]*

**B2 (E)** Rule 1.3 Comment 2 says a lawyer's workload should be controlled so that:
a) billable hours are maximized b) associates are fully utilized c) each matter can be handled competently d) partners can review every filing — **c.** Workload control is framed as a competence and diligence measure. *[ABA Model Rule 1.3, Comment 2]*

**B3 (M)** A solo practitioner has no arrangement for open matters if the lawyer dies or becomes disabled. Rule 1.3 Comment 5 recommends:
a) nothing, because the bar will step in b) closing every matter at year end c) posting a surety bond d) a plan designating another competent lawyer to review files, notify clients, and take protective action — **d.** Succession planning is treated as part of diligence for a sole practitioner. *[ABA Model Rule 1.3, Comment 5]*

**B4 (M)** A 40-lawyer firm's matter list holds matters where no one has confirmed whether the representation ended. Rule 1.3 Comment 4 advises that:
a) the lawyer should clarify in writing whether the relationship has ended, so the client does not mistakenly believe the lawyer is still watching the matter b) such matters stay open indefinitely c) matters close automatically after 90 days of inactivity d) a monitoring fee is billed until the client responds — **a.** Doubt about whether a relationship continues is resolved by clarifying it, preferably in writing. *[ABA Model Rule 1.3, Comment 4]*

**B5 (E)** A firm's docketing rule for federal periods stated in days should, under FRCP 6(a)(1):
a) skip weekends for periods shorter than 11 days b) count every day, including intermediate Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays c) count business days only d) exclude both the day of the event and the last day — **b.** The 2009 amendment removed the old under-11-day exception. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(a)(1)]*

**B6 (M)** A federal response deadline falls on a Sunday. Under FRCP 6(a)(1)(C) the period:
a) ends the preceding Friday b) ends Sunday at noon c) continues to run until the end of the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday d) is extended by seven days automatically — **c.** The last day rolls forward, never backward. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(a)(1)(C)]*

**B7 (M)** For electronic filing, FRCP 6(a)(4) says the last day of a period ends at:
a) 5:00 p.m. in the filer's time zone b) the clerk's office closing time c) midnight in the filer's time zone d) midnight in the court's time zone — **d.** A docketing system should carry the court's time zone, not the office's. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(a)(4)(A)]*

**B8 (H)** A litigation associate discovers that a federal deadline expired last week. Under FRCP 6(b)(1)(B), a motion to extend made after the time has expired requires a showing of:
a) excusable neglect b) good cause alone c) prejudice to the opposing party d) newly discovered evidence — **a.** Before expiration good cause suffices; after expiration the movant must also show excusable neglect. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(b)(1)]*

**B9 (H)** FRCP 6(b)(2) lists deadlines a court may not extend. Which one is on that list?
a) the Rule 26(f) conference date b) the Rule 59(b) time to move for a new trial c) the Rule 34 response period d) the Rule 12 answer period — **b.** Rules 50(b) and (d), 52(b), 59(b), (d) and (e), and 60(b) are excluded from extension. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(b)(2)]*

**B10 (M)** Under FRCP 6(d), three days are added to a response period when service is made:
a) electronically through the court's system b) in person c) by mail, by leaving with the clerk, or by other means consented to d) by any method — **c.** Electronic service was removed from the three-day rule in 2016. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(d), as amended 2016]*

**B11 (E)** Under FRCP 16(b)(2), a scheduling order must issue within the earlier of:
a) 30 days after the complaint is filed b) 120 days after service on the first defendant c) 14 days after the Rule 26(f) conference d) 90 days after any defendant has been served or 60 days after any defendant has appeared — **d.** A docket entry for the scheduling order can be computed from the earlier of those two events. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(b)(2)]*

**B12 (M)** FRCP 26(f)(1) requires the parties to confer as soon as practicable and in any event at least:
a) 21 days before a scheduling conference is to be held or a scheduling order is due b) 7 days before trial c) 60 days after the complaint is filed d) any time before discovery closes — **a.** The conference date backs off the Rule 16(b) date. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(f)(1)]*

**B13 (M)** After the Rule 26(f) conference, the parties' written report on the discovery plan is due within:
a) 7 days b) 14 days c) 21 days d) 30 days — **b.** The 14-day report date is a docketed item tied to the conference date. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(f)(2)]*

**B14 (H)** A supervising partner assigns a brief to an associate and a records project to a paralegal. Under Rules 5.1(b) and 5.3(b), the partner's duty as direct supervisor is to:
a) personally review every document either person produces b) countersign all of their time entries c) make reasonable efforts to ensure the person's conduct conforms to, or is compatible with, the lawyer's professional obligations d) delegate supervision to the office administrator — **c.** Both rules set a reasonable-efforts standard for a lawyer with direct supervisory authority. *[ABA Model Rule 5.1(b); Rule 5.3(b)]*

**B15 (E)** Rule 5.3 Comment 2 says nonlawyer staff such as paralegals and interns must be given:
a) a bonus plan tied to billable hours b) a bar examination waiver c) client contact only through a lawyer d) appropriate instruction and supervision on the ethical aspects of their work, particularly confidentiality — **d.** The comment notes that nonlawyers lack legal training and are not subject to discipline. *[ABA Model Rule 5.3, Comment 2]*

**B16 (M)** Rule 1.1 Comment 8 adds to the duty of competence a duty to keep abreast of:
a) the benefits and risks associated with relevant technology b) marketing trends in the firm's region c) changes in bar dues d) office lease terms — **a.** Technology competence was added by the 2012 amendments. *[ABA Model Rule 1.1, Comment 8]*

**B17 (M)** Green Book Principle 10 (design control activities), applied to a firm's docket, suggests:
a) relying on the responsible lawyer's memory b) a second-person verification of each deadline entry as a preventive control c) a year-end review only d) no controls at firms under 20 staff — **b.** Control activities are designed to reduce risk to an acceptable level; a two-person check on entry is a common docket design. *[GAO Green Book 2025, Principle 10, adapted to firm context]*

**B18 (H)** The 2025 Green Book revision states that management prioritizes which kind of control activity where appropriate?
a) detective controls b) corrective controls c) preventive control activities d) outsourced controls — **c.** The 2025 edition adds an express emphasis on preventive controls. *[GAO Green Book 2025, OV4.16]*

**B19 (E)** Green Book Principle 16 (perform monitoring activities), transferred to a firm's docket, looks like:
a) buying a calendar application b) hiring a docket clerk c) a monthly partner meeting with no agenda d) periodic review of the docket for missed, unassigned or duplicate deadlines — **d.** Monitoring is ongoing evaluation of whether controls are operating. *[GAO Green Book 2025, Principle 16, adapted to firm context]*

**B20 (M)** Rule 5.1(c)(2) makes a partner responsible for another lawyer's violation when the partner:
a) knew of the conduct at a time when its consequences could be avoided or mitigated and failed to take reasonable remedial action b) works in the same office c) shares fees with the lawyer d) is senior to the lawyer — **a.** Responsibility attaches to knowledge plus a failure to act, not to proximity or seniority. *[ABA Model Rule 5.1(c)(2)]*

**B21 (M)** A litigation firm's Rule 26(f) checklist includes a preservation discussion because Rule 26(f)(2) requires the parties to:
a) exchange expert reports b) discuss any issues about preserving discoverable information c) file a privilege log d) agree on a trial date — **b.** Preservation planning is tied to the early conference. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(f)(2)]*

**B22 (E)** ABA Formal Opinion 512 says supervisory lawyers should do what about subordinates' use of generative AI tools?
a) prohibit all use b) bill training time to the client c) train them on the basics, capabilities, limits, ethical issues and secure data handling, and monitor compliance d) nothing unless a client asks — **c.** Managerial lawyers set policy; supervisors train and monitor. *[ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024); Rules 5.1 and 5.3]*

## SECTION C — Time Capture, Billing, Collections and the Trust-Account Interface (23: 7E/12M/4H)

**C1 (E)** Rule 1.5(a) lists factors for the reasonableness of a fee. Which is among them?
a) the firm's overhead ratio b) the client's net worth c) the time and labor required and the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved d) the number of associates at the firm — **c.** Other listed factors include customary local charges, the amount involved and results, time limitations, and whether the fee is fixed or contingent. *[ABA Model Rule 1.5(a)]*

**C2 (M)** A firm raises a client's hourly rate mid-matter. Rule 1.5(b) treats a change in the basis or rate of the fee as something that:
a) needs no communication if under 10 percent b) requires a new conflict check c) requires court approval d) must also be communicated to the client — **d.** Changes in fee basis or rate are communicated like the original basis. *[ABA Model Rule 1.5(b)]*

**C3 (M)** An hourly lawyer at a small firm spends four hours in one sitting on matters for three clients. ABA Formal Opinion 93-379 says the lawyer may bill:
a) the actual time spent on each matter, not twelve hours in total b) four hours to each client c) twelve hours split evenly d) four hours to the largest client only — **a.** A lawyer may not bill more time than actually spent, and may not bill the same hours to more than one client. *[ABA Formal Opinion 93-379 (1993)]*

**C4 (H)** A firm reuses research it produced for a prior client and bills the new client the full original hours. ABA Formal Opinion 93-379 treats that as:
a) permitted if the rate is customary b) not permitted; the savings from recycled work product pass to the new client rather than rebilling the original hours c) permitted with a 10 percent discount d) permitted for flat-fee clients only — **b.** The opinion rejects billing the hours originally spent on work reused for another client. *[ABA Formal Opinion 93-379 (1993)]*

**C5 (M)** A firm marks up court reporter and courier invoices before passing them to clients. ABA Formal Opinion 93-379 says third-party disbursements are charged:
a) at cost plus 15 percent b) at any rate the firm chooses c) at actual cost, with no surcharge absent disclosure and agreement d) never to the client — **c.** Discounts received are passed on and surcharges require disclosure. *[ABA Formal Opinion 93-379 (1993)]*

**C6 (E)** Under ABA Formal Opinion 93-379, general office overhead is:
a) always billed as a separate line item b) billed at 10 percent of fees c) billed only to contingency clients d) absorbed in the fee unless separate charging was disclosed in advance — **d.** Overhead is not a separate charge without prior disclosure. *[ABA Formal Opinion 93-379 (1993)]*

**C7 (M)** A lawyer uses a generative AI tool that reduces a drafting task from three hours to one. Under ABA Formal Opinion 512, an hourly biller may charge:
a) only the time actually spent using the tool and reviewing its output b) the three hours the work would have taken c) a flat AI surcharge per document d) the time saved as an efficiency fee — **a.** Hourly billing covers time spent, not time saved. *[ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024); Rule 1.5]*

**C8 (M)** Under ABA Formal Opinion 512, time a lawyer spends learning a generative AI tool the lawyer will use regularly is:
a) billable at half rate b) not billable to a client, unless the client asked for a specific tool the lawyer has not mastered and agreed in advance to pay c) always billable as research d) billable to the first client who benefits — **b.** Learning a regularly used tool is basic competence, not client work. *[ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024); Rules 1.1 and 1.5]*

**C9 (E)** ABA Formal Opinion 505 describes an advance fee, including a flat fee paid before the work is done, as:
a) the lawyer's property on receipt b) firm revenue if labeled nonrefundable c) client property until earned, to be held in trust d) exempt from Rule 1.15 — **c.** Advance payments are safeguarded as client property. *[ABA Formal Opinion 505 (2023); Rule 1.15(c)]*

**C10 (M)** A firm's engagement letter calls the initial deposit a "nonrefundable retainer, earned on receipt." ABA Formal Opinion 505 says that label:
a) makes the fee earned at payment b) is required by Rule 1.5 c) is permitted in every state d) does not make the fee earned; the unearned portion is refunded on termination — **d.** The opinion suggests plain language such as "advance" or "deposit for fees." *[ABA Formal Opinion 505 (2023); Rule 1.16(d)]*

**C11 (H)** A California firm deposits a true flat fee into its operating account at engagement. California Rule 1.15(b) permits that only when:
a) the client receives written disclosure that the fee may be required to be held in trust until earned and that any unearned portion is refundable, and, for flat fees over $1,000, the agreement and disclosure are in a writing signed by the client b) the fee is under $5,000 c) the matter is transactional d) the firm has fewer than five lawyers — **a.** This is a California departure from Model Rule 1.15(c). *[Cal. Rules of Prof. Conduct 1.15(b); state-specific]*

**C12 (E)** Rule 1.15(c) says advance fees and expenses deposited in trust are withdrawn:
a) at the lawyer's discretion b) only as fees are earned or expenses incurred c) automatically at month end d) when the client requests it — **b.** Withdrawal follows earning, not the calendar. *[ABA Model Rule 1.15(c)]*

**C13 (M)** A client disputes part of a final invoice that the firm intends to pay from trust. Under Rule 1.15(e), the firm's procedure should:
a) transfer the whole invoice and resolve later b) refund the entire balance c) keep the disputed portion in trust until the dispute is resolved and promptly distribute the undisputed portion d) freeze every client balance in the account — **c.** Disputed funds stay separate; undisputed funds move promptly. *[ABA Model Rule 1.15(e)]*

**C14 (M)** A settlement check arrives at a plaintiff contingency firm. Rule 1.15(d) requires the firm to:
a) hold the funds for 30 days before any action b) invest the funds pending disbursement c) deposit the funds in the operating account d) promptly notify the client and any third person with an interest, promptly deliver what each is entitled to receive, and render a full accounting on request — **d.** Notice, delivery and accounting are the three duties on receipt. *[ABA Model Rule 1.15(d)]*

**C15 (H)** A firm keeps a $2,500 "cushion" of its own money in the trust account to avoid overdrafts. Rule 1.15(b) limits lawyer funds in trust to:
a) amounts needed to pay bank service charges, and only in that amount b) no lawyer funds under any circumstances c) any amount under $5,000 d) one month of anticipated fees — **a.** Several states cap the nominal amount by rule (for example Minnesota Appendix 1 at $200). *[ABA Model Rule 1.15(b); state caps vary, state-specific]*

**C16 (M)** A Minnesota firm's monthly trust close ties the check register, the total of the client subsidiary ledgers, and the bank statement. That procedure matches:
a) FRCP 26(g) certification b) the monthly three-way reconciliation required by Minnesota Rule 1.15(h) and Appendix 1 c) a Model Rule 1.15 text requirement d) the Florida annual listing — **b.** Model Rule 1.15 is silent on reconciliation frequency; states such as Minnesota and California require a monthly three-way reconciliation. *[Minnesota Rule 1.15(h) and Appendix 1; state-specific]*

**C17 (M)** A Florida firm's compliance calendar carries an annual trust accounting certificate. Under Florida Rule 5-1.2, that certificate is filed with the Bar:
a) by January 31 each year b) with each monthly reconciliation c) between June 1 and August 15 each year d) only when a trust account is opened — **c.** Florida also requires monthly reconciliation, an annual listing of client balances and six-year retention. *[Florida Rule 5-1.2; state-specific]*

**C18 (E)** Two firms share a fee on a referred matter. Rule 1.5(e) permits the division when:
a) the referring lawyer receives under 25 percent b) the client is not told c) both firms are in the same state d) the division is proportional to services or each lawyer assumes joint responsibility, the client agrees in a writing that states the share each lawyer receives, and the total fee is reasonable — **d.** All three conditions apply. *[ABA Model Rule 1.5(e)]*

**C19 (M)** The 2025 Legal Trends Report (REPORTED) benchmarks show a collection rate of 93 percent. In that framework, the figure means:

**C22 (H)** At a five-lawyer firm, one bookkeeper prepares trust disbursements, signs them under delegated authority, and performs the monthly reconciliation. Reading Green Book Principle 10 for a firm, the accepted response where full segregation is not feasible is:
a) accept the risk silently b) stop using a trust account c) hire until segregation is possible d) design a compensating control, such as a lawyer's review and sign-off of every reconciliation and of disbursements above a threshold, and document the trade-off — **d.** Principles apply with judgment; management documents how the objective is met when full segregation cannot be designed. ## SECTION D — Work Product, Document Handling, Records and Matter Closing (20: 5E/10M/5H)

**D1 (E)** A former client asks for the file. ABA Formal Opinion 471 lists materials that must be surrendered under Rule 1.16(d). Which set matches?
a) the firm's internal conflict memoranda only b) the firm's time and expense records only c) the firm's marketing file d) documents the client provided, correspondence, pleadings, discovery, and final versions of work product such as filed documents and expert reports — **d.** Those categories are the core of what a client is entitled to receive. *[ABA Formal Opinion 471 (2015); Rule 1.16(d)]*

**D2 (M)** Under ABA Formal Opinion 471, which materials may a firm generally withhold under the Model Rules, unless they are needed to protect the client's interests?
a) internal lawyer notes, drafts, conflict-check memoranda, legal research, and administrative materials b) pleadings filed with the court c) correspondence with opposing counsel d) documents the client provided — **a.** The opinion also says drafts and research must be furnished when a filing deadline is imminent and withholding would prejudice the client. *[ABA Formal Opinion 471 (2015); Rule 1.16(d)]*

**D3 (H)** ABA Formal Opinion 471 describes two jurisdictional approaches to file return. Under the "entire file" approach:
a) the client receives only finished work product b) the lawyer must return all file materials unless a specific exception applies, such as material that would harm a third party c) the lawyer keeps everything not filed with a court d) the client receives the file only after fees are paid — **b.** The "end product" approach limits the client to finished products; states differ. *[ABA Formal Opinion 471 (2015); state approaches differ, state-specific]*

**D4 (M)** A midsize firm is designing a file destruction procedure. ABA Informal Opinion 1384 describes practices such as:
a) destroying all files seven years after opening b) keeping every file permanently c) screening files before destruction, keeping an index of destroyed files, protecting confidentiality in the method of destruction, and not destroying client property or originals d) shredding without review once storage is full — **c.** The opinion sets no fixed period but names the safeguards. *[ABA Informal Opinion 1384 (1977); CITED via state bar summaries]*

**D5 (H)** A firm with offices in Minnesota, Florida and New York sets a single trust-record retention period equal to the Model Rule's five years after termination. The gap in that design is that:
a) the Model Rule requires ten years b) trust records need no retention after the account closes c) the Model Rule period runs from matter opening d) the Model Rule's five years is a model default and the states examined set longer periods (Minnesota six, Florida six, New York seven years) — **d.** State rules set the operative number and trigger. *[ABA Model Rule 1.15(a); Minnesota Rule 1.15(h), Florida Rule 5-1.2, New York Rule 1.15(d); state-specific]*

**D6 (E)** The Sedona Commentary on Legal Holds, Guideline 1, places the trigger for the duty to preserve at the point when an organization:
a) reasonably anticipates litigation b) is served with a complaint c) receives a discovery request d) hires litigation counsel — **a.** A firm's hold procedure starts at reasonable anticipation, which can precede filing. *[Sedona Conference Commentary on Legal Holds, Second Edition (2019), Guideline 1; CITED]*

**D7 (M)** Sedona Legal Hold Guideline 8 describes an effective hold notice as one that:
a) is sent to the general counsel only b) identifies custodians, explains what to do, obtains acknowledgment, addresses system-level data, and is followed by periodic reminders c) is issued orally to save time d) covers only email — **b.** Acknowledgment and reminders are the parts most often skipped. *[Sedona Conference Commentary on Legal Holds, Second Edition (2019), Guideline 8; CITED]*

**D8 (M)** A firm's hold procedure has no step for lifting a hold. Sedona Legal Hold Guideline 11 addresses this by recommending:
a) holds remain in force permanently b) holds expire automatically after one year c) a release procedure so that normal retention resumes when the duty ends d) destruction of held data at case close — **c.** Release is part of a complete hold process. *[Sedona Conference Commentary on Legal Holds, Second Edition (2019), Guideline 11; CITED]*

**D9 (H)** A client's ESI was lost because an auto-delete setting was not suspended after a hold should have issued. Under FRCP 37(e)(2), an adverse-inference instruction is available only on a finding that:
a) the loss was negligent b) the loss was grossly negligent c) the information was relevant d) the party acted with the intent to deprive another party of the information's use in the litigation — **d.** The 2015 amendment rejected adverse inferences based on negligence alone. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e)(2) and 2015 Committee Note]*

**D10 (M)** Under FRCP 37(e)(1), when lost ESI prejudices another party but there is no intent to deprive, the court may order:
a) measures no greater than necessary to cure the prejudice b) dismissal of the action c) a mandatory adverse presumption d) default judgment — **a.** Curative measures need only prejudice; the severe sanctions need intent. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e)(1)]*

**D11 (E)** A privileged email is produced by mistake. Under FRE 502(b), the disclosure is not a waiver if:
a) the opposing party agrees to return it b) the disclosure was inadvertent, the holder took reasonable steps to prevent it, and the holder promptly took reasonable steps to rectify the error c) the document is marked confidential d) the email was sent by a paralegal — **b.** All three elements are required. *[Fed. R. Evid. 502(b)]*

**D12 (M)** A litigation team's protocol asks the court for a FRE 502(d) order at the outset of discovery. The reason is that a 502(d) order:
a) shortens the discovery period b) waives the privilege log requirement c) makes a non-waiver ruling binding in all other federal and state proceedings, whereas a party agreement alone binds only the parties unless incorporated in an order d) replaces the need for privilege review — **c.** 502(d) and 502(e) distinguish court orders from private agreements. *[Fed. R. Evid. 502(d) and (e)]*

**D13 (H)** The FRE 502 Committee Note identifies what as relevant to whether a producing party took reasonable steps to prevent disclosure?
a) the number of lawyers on the case b) whether the client is an individual c) the size of the opposing party d) the use of advanced analytical software and linguistic tools in privilege screening and the efficiency of the party's records management system — **d.** A documented review protocol and records system are the evidentiary record for the 502(b) showing. *[Fed. R. Evid. 502, Committee Note]*

**D14 (M)** A firm receives notice under FRCP 26(b)(5)(B) that a produced document is privileged. The receiving firm's procedure should:
a) promptly return, sequester or destroy the document and any copies, not use or disclose it until the claim is resolved, and, if it contests the claim, present it to the court under seal b) read it fully before responding c) forward it to the client for evaluation d) keep it until the producing party moves for a protective order — **a.** The clawback rule sets the receiving party's duties. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(5)(B)]*

**D15 (E)** A firm's pre-filing redaction check for federal filings follows FRCP 5.2(a). The rule allows a filing to include only:
a) the full Social Security number if the client consents b) the last four digits of a Social Security or taxpayer identification number, the year of birth, a minor's initials, and the last four digits of a financial account number c) the full date of birth and full account number d) nothing about the individual at all — **b.** Sealing, protective orders and a sealed reference list are the alternatives when more is needed. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 5.2(a), (d), (g)]*

**D16 (M)** Under FRCP 5(d)(3)(C), a filing made through a person's electronic-filing account and authorized by that person, with that person's name on the signature block:
a) requires a scanned wet signature b) is unsigned until the clerk stamps it c) constitutes that person's signature d) must also be signed by local counsel — **c.** The rule ties the signature to the account holder's authorization, which is why account credentials are treated as personal. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 5(d)(3)(C)]*

**D17 (H)** A firm's collection workflow records a hash value for every copied data set and has the litigation support professional sign a certification. That workflow is built to use:
a) FRE 803(6) business records b) FRCP 34(b) form of production c) FRE 502(d) d) FRE 902(14), which makes data copied from a device or file self-authenticating when authenticated by digital identification and accompanied by a qualified person's certification with reasonable written notice — **d.** The 2017 Committee Note describes hash-value matching as the usual method. *[Fed. R. Evid. 902(14) and 2017 Committee Note]*

**D18 (M)** A firm stores executed engagement letters only as PDF copies. Under ESIGN section 7001(d), an electronic record satisfies a legal retention requirement when it:
a) accurately reflects the information in the original and remains accessible to those entitled to access it, in a form capable of accurate reproduction for later reference b) is printed once a year c) is stored on the firm's own server d) is signed by two witnesses — **a.** Accuracy and continued accessibility are the tests. *[15 U.S.C. 7001(d); ESIGN Act]*

**D19 (E)** Under FRCP 34(b)(2)(E), when a request does not specify a form, ESI is produced:
a) in paper only b) in a form in which it is ordinarily maintained or in a reasonably usable form, and not in more than one form c) in every available format d) as screenshots — **b.** The rule also lets the responding party state the form it intends to use. *[Fed. R. Civ. P. 34(b)(2)(D) and (E)]*

**D20 (M)** At closing, a firm transmits a copy of the file to the client by email. Rule 1.6 Comment 19 says the lawyer must take reasonable precautions to prevent the information from reaching unintended recipients, and that:
a) encryption is always required b) email may never be used for closed files c) special security measures are not required if the method affords a reasonable expectation of privacy, though special circumstances may warrant more d) the client must sign a waiver first — **c.** The transmission standard is reasonable precautions, judged by the circumstances. *[ABA Model Rule 1.6, Comment 19]*
