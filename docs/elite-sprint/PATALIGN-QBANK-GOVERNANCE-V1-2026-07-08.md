## SECTION A — Control Environment & Firm Governance (27: 8E/14M/5H)

**A1 (E)** The foundation of any internal control system, per the Green Book's first component, is:
a) monitoring software b) the control environment — the tone and discipline leadership sets c) segregation of duties d) an annual audit — **b.** All other components rest on leadership's demonstrated commitment to integrity. *[Green Book 2025, Component: Control Environment]*

**A2 (E)** "Tone at the top" refers to:
a) the loudest partner b) leadership's demonstrated commitment to integrity and ethical values c) the firm's marketing voice d) client communication style — **b.** Principle 1: the oversight body and management demonstrate commitment to integrity and ethical values. *[Green Book 2025, Principle 1]*

**A3 (E)** A written code of conduct is most effective when it is:
a) filed with the state board b) communicated, acknowledged, and enforced with documented consequences c) posted on the website d) reviewed only at hiring — **b.** Standards of conduct must be established, communicated, and adherence evaluated. *[Green Book 2025, P1 attributes]*

**A4 (E)** Under Circular 230, a practitioner who oversees a firm's tax practice must:
a) personally review every return b) take reasonable steps to ensure the firm has adequate procedures for compliance c) hold an EA credential d) file annual conflict statements with the IRS — **b.** §10.36 places responsibility for firm-wide compliance procedures on those with principal authority. *[Circular 230 §10.36]*

**A5 (E)** Assigning authority and responsibility so that key duties are clearly owned addresses which Green Book principle?
a) P3 — establish structure, authority, and responsibility b) P8 — fraud risk c) P11 — IT controls d) P16 — ongoing evaluations — **a.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 3]*

**A6 (E)** Competence, per the Green Book, means the firm:
a) hires only CPAs b) attracts, develops, and retains individuals able to fulfill assigned responsibilities c) outsources difficult work d) requires annual reviews — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 4]*

**A7 (E)** Which is the best evidence a firm actually enforces accountability?
a) an org chart b) documented performance consequences tied to control responsibilities c) a mission statement d) partner tenure — **b.** P5: hold individuals accountable for their internal control responsibilities. *[Green Book 2025, Principle 5]*

**A8 (E)** Under GAGAS, before accepting work a practitioner must evaluate independence threats. "Familiarity threat" means:
a) knowing the standards too well b) a relationship so close that objectivity is compromised c) repeating the same engagement type d) using familiar software — **b.** *[Yellow Book, GAGAS independence conceptual framework]*

**A9 (M)** A managing partner overrides a documented billing control "just this once" at year-end. The primary control-environment damage is:
a) the dollar amount involved b) the demonstrated message that controls yield to convenience, undermining P1 c) the client's perception d) none, if disclosed — **b.** Management override signals that standards are negotiable — the most corrosive single act against tone at the top. *[Green Book 2025, P1; COSO cited]*

**A10 (M)** A 12-person firm cannot fully segregate duties. The Green Book's answer is:
a) ignore segregation below 20 staff b) design compensating controls — e.g., partner review of unsegregated functions — and document the trade-off c) hire until segregation is possible d) outsource bookkeeping — **b.** Principles apply with judgment; management documents how objectives are met when full design isn't feasible. *[Green Book 2025, application guidance]*

**A11 (M)** Who should own the firm's internal control system, per the Green Book model adapted to a firm?
a) the youngest partner b) an oversight body/managing partner distinct from day-to-day process owners where feasible c) the IT vendor d) each client — **b.** P2: the oversight body oversees the system separately from management's operation of it. *[Green Book 2025, Principle 2]*

**A12 (M)** Circular 230 §10.33 "best practices" advises practitioners to:
a) guarantee outcomes b) communicate clearly, establish relevant facts, and act fairly with integrity before the IRS c) minimize documentation d) avoid written advice — **b.** *[Circular 230 §10.33]*

**A13 (M)** A firm lists "quality" as a value but promotes exclusively on realization rates. Per P4/P5, the risk is:
a) none — billing matters b) incentives contradict stated values, teaching staff which standard is real c) slower promotions d) higher CPE costs — **b.** Accountability structures must align with control objectives, not against them. *[Green Book 2025, P4-P5]*

**A14 (M)** The 2025 Green Book added emphasis in its appendixes on which three risk areas?
a) marketing, HR, tax b) fraud, improper payments, and information security c) leasing, revenue, inventory d) mergers, staffing, PR — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Appendix II-III; GAO-25-107721]*

**A15 (M)** Under GAGAS, a "self-review threat" arises when:
a) reviewing your own prior work product as part of an engagement b) reviewing a peer's work c) clients review the firm d) using checklists — **a.** *[Yellow Book, independence threats]*

**A16 (M)** The firm's organizational documentation (who reports to whom, who approves what) is required by:
a) P3 — structure, authority, responsibility b) P13 — quality information c) state law only d) it's optional — **a.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 3]*

**A17 (M)** A new partner inherits a book of business with undocumented processes. The FIRST control-environment step is:
a) re-price the book b) document key roles and approval authorities for those clients c) fire non-compliant staff d) buy software — **b.** Authority and responsibility must be defined before controls can operate. *[Green Book 2025, P3]*

**A18 (M)** Which scenario best demonstrates Principle 2 (oversight) in a small firm?
a) the same partner performs and approves all billing adjustments b) a rotating second-partner review of control exceptions each quarter c) annual self-certification d) trusting tenure — **b.** Oversight requires separation from operation, scaled to size. *[Green Book 2025, P2]*

**A19 (M)** Per Circular 230 §10.22, "diligence as to accuracy" is satisfied when a practitioner:
a) personally verifies every fact b) may rely on another's work product with reasonable care in engaging, supervising, and evaluating it c) has E&O insurance d) uses IRS transcripts only — **b.** *[Circular 230 §10.22(b)]*

**A20 (M)** Staff hesitate to report a partner's control override. Which principle failed?
a) P14 — internal communication, including separate reporting lines b) P11 — ITGC c) P6 — objectives d) P9 — change — **a.** Effective internal communication requires channels that bypass the chain when the chain is the problem. *[Green Book 2025, P14]*

**A21 (M)** The strongest indicator that a firm's ethics program is real rather than paper:
a) length of the manual b) documented instances where the firm accepted cost to uphold a standard (fired a client, reversed a bill) c) a signed acknowledgment file d) an ethics poster — **b.** Adherence is evaluated by actions and deviations addressed — evidence over artifacts. *[Green Book 2025, P1]*

**A22 (H)** A firm's largest client (22% of revenue) pressures a manager to reclassify expenses. The manager complies; a partner discovers it post-issuance. Rank the control-environment failures in causal order:
a) P1 tone → P5 accountability → P14 communication b) client concentration (P7 risk) created undue-influence threat (GAGAS); absent counter-pressure procedures (P1/P12) enabled it; weak escalation channels (P14) hid it; the response now tests P5 c) P11 → P16 → P17 d) only the manager failed — **b.** The full causal chain runs risk → threat → control absence → communication gap → accountability test. *[Green Book 2025 P1/P5/P7/P12/P14; Yellow Book undue-influence threat]*

**A23 (H)** Under GAGAS's conceptual framework, when a threat to independence is identified, the required sequence is:
a) withdraw immediately b) evaluate significance; if significant, apply safeguards; if safeguards can't reduce the threat to acceptable, decline/withdraw; document throughout c) disclose to the client and proceed d) obtain a waiver — **b.** *[Yellow Book, GAGAS 3.x conceptual framework]*

**A24 (H)** A firm adopts an AI tool that drafts client deliverables. Under §10.36 + P4, the governance gap MOST likely to bite first is:
a) the tool's cost b) no defined competence/review standard for AI-assisted output — responsibility assigned to no one c) branding d) storage location — **b.** New capability without assigned oversight authority is a P3/P4 failure that §10.36 makes the principals' problem. *[Circular 230 §10.36; Green Book 2025 P3-P4]*

**A25 (H)** Two founding partners disagree on a revenue-recognition policy for the firm's own books, and staff receive conflicting instructions. The Green Book diagnosis:
a) P6 failure — objectives not clearly defined and communicated, cascading into P3 authority confusion b) P8 fraud c) P17 remediation d) P15 external communication — **a.** Objectives precede structure; ambiguity at the top makes every downstream control unanchored. *[Green Book 2025, P6/P3]*

**A26 (H)** Which combination most reliably predicts control-environment decay in an acquisitive firm (per COSO-adapted research and the Green Book's change principle)?
a) rapid headcount growth + unharmonized policies + retained legacy approval authorities b) new letterhead c) higher CPE spend d) more partners — **a.** P9: significant change requires reassessing the control system; acquisitions multiply P3 conflicts. *[Green Book 2025, P9; COSO cited]*

**A27 (H)** A practitioner with principal authority learns an associate gave willfully misleading written tax advice a year ago. Under Circular 230, the firm exposure turns on:
a) whether the advice was oral b) whether reasonable compliance procedures existed and whether, upon knowing, prompt corrective action was taken (§10.36(b)) c) the client's size d) e-signature use — **b.** *[Circular 230 §10.36]*

## SECTION B — Risk Assessment & Vendor Risk (23: 7E/12M/4H)

**B1 (E)** The Green Book's precondition for risk assessment is:
a) an ERM department b) clearly defined objectives (P6) — you can't assess risk to undefined goals c) insurance d) a heat map — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 6]*

**B2 (E)** Risk identification (P7) should consider:
a) only financial risks b) internal and external risks to objectives, analyzed for likelihood and impact c) only fraud d) only IT — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 7]*

**B3 (E)** Fraud risk assessment (P8) explicitly considers:
a) incentives/pressures, opportunity, and rationalization b) only prior frauds c) only cash handling d) external actors only — **a.** The fraud triangle is baked into the principle's attributes. *[Green Book 2025, Principle 8]*

**B4 (E)** NIST CSF 2.0 added a sixth function to the original five. It is:
a) Govern b) Encrypt c) Insure d) Archive — **a.** GOVERN covers risk strategy, roles, policy, and supply-chain oversight. *[NIST CSF 2.0]*

**B5 (E)** "Supply chain risk" for a CPA firm most directly includes:
a) office supplies b) the software vendors and processors that touch client data c) commuting patterns d) furniture leases — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0, GV.SC]*

**B6 (E)** Significant change (new service line, merger, new platform) triggers which Green Book response?
a) nothing if revenue holds b) reassess risks and adjust the control system (P9) c) a press release d) rebranding — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 9]*

**B7 (E)** Client concentration above roughly 15-20% of revenue is primarily a risk because:
a) billing complexity b) it creates undue-influence pressure on judgment and reduces the firm's ability to walk away c) software licensing d) scheduling — **b.** *[Yellow Book undue-influence threat; Green Book P7 applied]*

**B8 (M)** A firm inventories its vendors and finds 23 tools with client-data access, 9 with no contract on file. The FIRST remediation, per GV.SC:
a) cancel all nine b) establish the supplier inventory as authoritative, then risk-tier vendors by data sensitivity and remediate contracts by tier c) encrypt everything d) switch to one vendor — **b.** Governance precedes controls: know, tier, then contract. *[NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC; Green Book P7]*

**B9 (M)** The right cadence for reassessing a firm's risk register, per the Green Book's design intent:
a) never — set once b) periodically AND on significant change, with the assessment documented c) only after incidents d) each audit season automatically suffices — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P7/P9]*

**B10 (M)** Which vendor question matters MOST when a bookkeeping platform holds client credentials?
a) UI colors b) its own access controls, breach-notification terms, and SOC 2 / equivalent assurance c) headquarters city d) mascot quality — **b.** Third-party assurance substitutes for controls you can't observe. *[NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC; Green Book P11 applied]*

**B11 (M)** A firm's fraud risk assessment ignores partners ("we trust each other"). The Green Book flaw:
a) none — trust is a control b) P8 requires assessing opportunity including management override, precisely where trust concentrates c) partners can't commit fraud d) it's a P13 issue — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P8]*

**B12 (M)** Ranking risks by "likelihood × impact" and accepting some consciously is called:
a) negligence b) risk response — accept/avoid/reduce/share, chosen deliberately and documented c) denial d) hedging — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P7 attributes]*

**B13 (M)** A vendor suffers a breach affecting three of the firm's clients. Contractually, the firm's exposure turns FIRST on:
a) the vendor's apology b) breach-notification obligations and data-processing terms the firm did (or didn't) negotiate c) the firm's logo license d) social media response — **b.** GV.SC: supply-chain risk is managed through agreements before incidents. *[NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC]*

**B14 (M)** Onboarding a new offshore processing vendor, which trio forms the minimum diligence set?
a) price, speed, references b) data-access scope, security assurance (SOC 2/ISO or equivalent), and exit/data-return terms c) time zone, language, size d) demo quality — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC; Green Book P7]*

**B15 (M)** "Risk appetite" in a firm context means:
a) how much work partners want b) the amount and type of risk leadership consciously accepts in pursuit of objectives, set at the top c) client hunger d) marketing budget — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P6-P7; COSO ERM cited]*

**B16 (M)** The change-risk (P9) blind spot most common in growing firms:
a) new hires b) processes that silently scaled past their controls — the approval built for 5 staff now "covering" 25 c) office moves d) fee schedules — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P9]*

**B17 (M)** Which fraud scheme does client-payment-by-firm-portal MOST newly enable, requiring P8 reassessment?
a) timesheet padding b) payment-redirection/business-email-compromise targeting the portal's remittance instructions c) stapler theft d) CPE fraud — **b.** New payment rails create new opportunity legs of the triangle. *[Green Book 2025, P8; NIST CSF 2.0 PR]*

**B18 (M)** A risk register lists "cybersecurity" once, unowned. The two missing properties that make a register real:
a) color coding and fonts b) a named owner and a defined response with review date c) length and confidentiality d) software and dashboards — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P7; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.RR]*

**B19 (M)** Vendor risk tiering should be driven primarily by:
a) contract value b) sensitivity and volume of data/access the vendor holds, and criticality to operations c) vendor size d) renewal date — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC]*

**B20 (H)** A firm adds a client-facing AI assistant with access to firm knowledge content. Construct the correct P7/P8/GV risk stack:
a) there is none if a vendor hosts it b) new data-flow risk (what the assistant can retrieve), new fraud/injection opportunity (prompt manipulation), new supplier dependence (model provider), each needing owner, response, and monitoring hooks c) only uptime risk d) only cost risk — **b.** Novel capability = novel risk inventory across all three lenses. *[Green Book 2025 P7-P8; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC/PR]*

**B21 (H)** Under GAGAS, a firm providing both bookkeeping and attest-adjacent review for the same client faces which threat pairing, and the primary safeguard is:
a) familiarity + advertising; disclaimer b) self-review + management participation; separate personnel/engagement teams and documented evaluation of significance, or decline c) undue influence only; fee increase d) none if immaterial — **b.** *[Yellow Book, GAGAS nonaudit services + conceptual framework]*

**B22 (H)** The 2025 Green Book's information-security emphasis implies which shift for firm risk registers relative to 2014 practice:
a) infosec is IT's problem b) information security risks are management's internal-control risks — assessed within P7/P8/P11 with documented control activities, not delegated wholesale to a vendor or department c) buy more insurance d) annual pen-test suffices — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Appendix II-III; GAO-25-107721]*

**B23 (H)** A firm's risk response to vendor concentration (one platform runs tax, books, and documents) is to "accept." For that acceptance to be defensible under P7, it must include:
a) nothing — acceptance is acceptance b) documented analysis of impact/likelihood, exit-cost awareness, contingency plan for vendor failure, leadership sign-off, and periodic revisit c) a longer contract d) faith in the vendor's size — **b.** Conscious acceptance is a documented decision with an owner and expiry, not a shrug. *[Green Book 2025, P7/P9]*

## SECTION C — Control Activities, Access & IT (22: 7E/11M/4H)

**C1 (E)** Control activities (P10) are:
a) audits b) the policies and procedures that enforce management's directives at process level c) meetings d) values statements — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 10]*

**C2 (E)** The classic control trio is prevention, detection, and:
a) punishment b) correction c) insurance d) denial — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Appendix II]*

**C3 (E)** Least-privilege access means:
a) partners get everything b) each person gets the minimum access needed for their role, reviewed periodically c) IT decides socially d) everyone shares one login — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA; Green Book P11]*

**C4 (E)** Shared logins in a firm's tax software primarily destroy:
a) speed b) accountability — actions can't be attributed to individuals (P5, PR.AA) c) branding d) storage — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA; Green Book P5/P11]*

**C5 (E)** Multi-factor authentication on client-data systems is best classified as:
a) detective b) preventive access control c) corrective d) optional — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA]*

**C6 (E)** IT general controls (P11) cover:
a) only hardware b) security management, access, configuration/change management, and contingency planning over technology c) websites d) printer maintenance — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 11]*

**C7 (E)** Documented policies (P12) matter because:
a) regulators like paper b) controls exist as management directives only when established in policy, with responsibilities assigned and timely execution expected c) they fill shelves d) marketing uses them — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 12]*

**C8 (M)** An employee departs. The control set that MUST fire same-day:
a) farewell email b) access revocation across all systems (including vendor SaaS), device recovery, and credential rotation for anything shared c) COBRA notice only d) exit interview — **b.** Offboarding is the highest-frequency access-control failure in small firms. *[NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA; Green Book P11]*

**C9 (M)** Segregation of duties in billing minimally separates:
a) preparing invoices, approving adjustments/write-offs, and recording receipts b) typing and printing c) email and phone d) tax and audit — **a.** *[Green Book 2025, P10 attributes]*

**C10 (M)** A quarterly user-access review is which control type, and its evidence is:
a) preventive; the policy b) detective; the signed review record listing accounts checked and exceptions actioned c) corrective; the apology d) none — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA; Green Book P16 applied]*

**C11 (M)** Client portals replace email attachments primarily to control:
a) fonts b) data-in-transit exposure and uncontrolled copies of client PII c) tone d) read receipts — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0 PR.DS]*

**C12 (M)** Change management (ITGC) for the firm's practice-management platform means:
a) any admin can toggle settings b) configuration changes are requested, approved, tested where material, and logged with who/what/when c) the vendor decides d) changes happen at night — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P11; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.PS]*

**C13 (M)** The control gap in "our vendor backs everything up":
a) none b) unverified recovery — backups without periodic restore testing and defined RTO/RPO are hope, not contingency planning c) cost d) speed — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P11 contingency; NIST CSF 2.0 RC]*

**C14 (M)** Write-off authority set at "any partner, any amount" fails which design test?
a) none — partners are trusted b) P10 proportionality: authorization thresholds should scale with risk, with elevated amounts requiring second approval c) speed d) client service — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P10]*

**C15 (M)** For remote staff, the compensating control set that MOST maps to PR.AA/PR.DS:
a) trust and quarterly visits b) MFA + managed/encrypted devices + VPN or zero-trust access + screen-lock policy c) longer passwords only d) printed procedures — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0 PR]*

**C16 (M)** A preparer discovers they can edit e-file authorization records after partner sign-off. This is a failure of:
a) nothing — convenient b) application-level access design: post-approval immutability/locking (P11) and P10 segregation c) training d) the IRS — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P10-P11]*

**C17 (M)** The minimum evidence that a documented control OPERATED (not just existed):
a) the policy PDF b) execution artifacts — signatures, timestamps, logs, exception lists for the period under review c) staff memory d) vendor brochures — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P12/P16; Yellow Book evidence standards]*

**C18 (M)** Data classification ("client PII / firm-internal / public") earns its keep by:
a) impressing clients b) letting controls scale with sensitivity — encryption, retention, and access rules keyed to class rather than uniform friction c) coloring folders d) nothing — **b.** *[NIST CSF 2.0 PR.DS; Green Book P13]*

**C19 (H)** Design the minimal effective control stack for firm-initiated client payments (e.g., payroll or tax drafts), combining P10 + PR.AA:
a) one partner does it all fast b) dual authorization with distinct credentials, out-of-band verification of any bank-detail change, immutable payment logs, and same-day reconciliation to source authorization c) email approval d) vendor handles it — **b.** Payment redirection is the top small-firm loss vector; each element closes one leg. *[Green Book 2025 P10; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA; Appendix II]*

**C20 (H)** An ITGC deficiency (weak change management at the platform level) undermines application controls downstream. The audit-logic reason:
a) it doesn't b) application controls rely on configuration integrity; if changes are uncontrolled, every automated control's continuity is unprovable for the period c) apps are separate d) only manual controls matter — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P11; Yellow Book IT controls logic]*

**C21 (H)** The firm adopts an AI assistant with retrieval from firm content. The P11/PR control that MUST precede launch:
a) a fun name b) retrieval-scope enforcement at the data layer (role/tenant filters in the query path) with tests proving out-of-scope content cannot be returned c) a disclaimer d) a beta label — **b.** Prompt-level rules are hints; data-layer scoping is the control. *[NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA/PR.DS; Green Book P11]*

**C22 (H)** A control costs more than the exposure it prevents. Green Book-consistent response:
a) keep it — controls are sacred b) redesign or replace it — control precision should be proportionate to risk, and management documents the cost-risk judgment c) hide it d) double it — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P10 design attributes]*

## SECTION D — Information, Communication & Monitoring (18: 5E/9M/4H)

**D1 (E)** Quality information (P13) must be:
a) plentiful b) appropriate, current, complete, accurate, accessible, and timely for its purpose c) confidential d) graphical — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 13]*

**D2 (E)** Internal communication (P14) includes, at minimum:
a) a newsletter b) communicating control responsibilities down AND providing channels (including alternative/anonymous ones) for information to flow up c) birthday cards d) slack emojis — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 14]*

**D3 (E)** External communication (P15) covers:
a) ads only b) two-way channels with clients, vendors, and regulators for control-relevant matters (complaints, breach notices, confirmations) c) social media d) sponsorships — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 15]*

**D4 (E)** Ongoing monitoring (P16) differs from separate evaluations because it is:
a) annual b) built into routine operations — reconciliations, exception reports, review cadences c) outsourced d) optional — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 16]*

**D5 (E)** When monitoring finds a deficiency, P17 requires:
a) filing it b) timely remediation by responsible parties, tracked to closure c) denial d) a meeting — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, Principle 17]*

**D6 (M)** A dashboard shows "firm alignment 73" with no source, date, or denominator. Its P13 defect:
a) ugly font b) the number lacks the attributes (source, currency, completeness) that make information usable for control decisions c) too precise d) too round — **b.** Every metric needs its unit and provenance — counts wearing the wrong noun corrupt decisions. *[Green Book 2025, P13]*

**D7 (M)** The firm's breach-notification duties to clients are which principle in action, and their trigger should be defined:
a) P15; in advance, in the incident-response plan and engagement terms — not improvised mid-incident b) P14; after legal review only c) P16; never d) P13; when convenient — **a.** *[Green Book 2025, P15; NIST CSF 2.0 RS.CO]*

**D8 (M)** An exception report that nobody is assigned to read is:
a) still a control b) not a control — monitoring requires an owner, a cadence, and evidence of follow-up (P16/P17) c) automation d) fine if archived — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P16-P17]*

**D9 (M)** Staff learn of policy changes via rumor. The P14 fix:
a) more rumors b) a defined channel-of-record for control-relevant communication with acknowledgment for material changes c) longer meetings d) posters — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P14]*

**D10 (M)** The correct treatment of a repeated finding (same deficiency two review cycles running):
a) re-log it b) escalate — recurrence means the P17 remediation was ineffective and the root cause or ownership was wrong c) close it as known d) rename it — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P17]*

**D11 (M)** Client complaint patterns belong in the control system as:
a) marketing data b) P15 external input feeding P7 risk assessment and P16 monitoring — complaints are free detective controls c) HR issues d) noise — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P15/P16]*

**D12 (M)** For a control-relevant metric to survive audit scrutiny, its definition must be:
a) flexible per meeting b) fixed, documented, and versioned — same numerator/denominator every period, changes disclosed c) partner-adjustable d) vendor-supplied — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P13; Yellow Book evidence]*

**D13 (M)** Monitoring the monitors ("who reviews the reviewer?") is addressed by:
a) infinite regress — impossible b) periodic separate evaluations (P16's second arm) — internal spot audits, peer review, or external assessment on a cycle c) trust d) rotation only — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P16]*

**D14 (M)** An anonymous reporting channel's effectiveness is evidenced by:
a) its existence b) usage data, documented investigations, and visible non-retaliation outcomes c) a poster d) the vendor contract — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P14 attributes]*

**D15 (H)** A firm's quarterly control report to partners shows all green for six straight quarters while staff turnover doubles and two clients churn citing errors. The P13/P16 diagnosis:
a) great controls b) the monitoring system measures the wrong signals or filters bad news — information quality failure masking control decay; redesign metrics against outcomes, not activity c) coincidence d) staffing issue only — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P13/P16]*

**D16 (H)** Integrating vendor-incident feeds (status pages, breach notices) into firm monitoring operationalizes which cross-framework pairing?
a) P15 external communication + GV.SC supply-chain monitoring, feeding P17 response when a vendor event touches firm data b) P12 + marketing c) P6 + pricing d) none — **a.** *[Green Book 2025, P15/P17; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC/DE]*

**D17 (H)** Design the minimum monitoring rhythm for a 15-person firm (Green Book-proportionate), choosing the best set:
a) annual everything b) daily: payment/exception queues · weekly: unbilled/WIP + access anomalies · monthly: reconciliations + open-deficiency review · quarterly: access recert + control self-assessment · annually: separate evaluation + risk-register refresh c) whenever convenient d) outsource all of it — **b.** Cadence scales with risk velocity, not headcount vanity. *[Green Book 2025, P16; Appendix II]*

**D18 (H)** A deficiency remediation changes a control that three other processes depend on. P17-consistent execution requires:
a) fix fast and quietly b) impact analysis across dependent processes, coordinated change (P11 change management), re-test of the dependent controls, and closure evidence for all touched paths — shared components need sibling verification, not spot fixes c) email the fix d) defer to year-end — **b.** *[Green Book 2025, P17/P11]*
